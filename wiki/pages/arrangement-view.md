# Arrangement view

`Arrangement` is the timeline: track cards on the left, clip lanes on the right, then, pinned under the lanes that scroll, the block of [return tracks](return-tracks.md#in-the-arrangement) (since `5632644`), the [main track](main-track.md) row and the [time row](#the-time-row) at the foot. It is one class defined across sixteen translation units (as of 2026-10-27), all in `native/src/ui/arrangement/` since 2026-10-13 with `ArrangementGrid.h`, `MixerSlider.h` (the card's fader and pan bars, which take a right-click for themselves), `MonitorSelector.*` and `SendKnob.*` beside them ([The source tree mirrors the layers](source-tree-mirrors-the-layers.md)): `Arrangement.cpp` (keys, zoom, split, playhead), `ArrangementGeometry.cpp` (lanes, time to x, snapping, hit zones), `ArrangementPainter.cpp` (rows, grid, rulers, the selection) and `ArrangementClipPainter.cpp` (every clip, piece by piece; split off on 2026-10-23, leaving the painter at 631 lines), `ArrangementSync.cpp` (rebuilding views and card controls from the edit), `ArrangementGestures.cpp` (pointer, card drags, menus, autoscroll, middle-drag pan), `ArrangementSelection.cpp` (region and clipboard), `ArrangementTrackClipboard.cpp` (the clipboard commands on whole cards, [Track clipboard](track-clipboard.md)), `ArrangementAutomation.cpp`, `ArrangementAutomationPaint.cpp` and `ArrangementAutomationChooser.cpp` (the card's automation chooser, 2026-10-25), `ArrangementMixerAutomation.cpp` (the fader and pan bars' automation menu and their following of a lane, commit `fd03386`, [Track automation](automation.md#the-cards-fader-and-pan)), `ArrangementGroups.cpp`, `ArrangementRename.cpp`, `ArrangementDrops.cpp` and `ArrangementBounce.cpp` (Ctrl+B, [Bounce to New Track](bounce-to-new-track.md), and the clip menu's [Slice to New MIDI Track](slice-to-new-midi-track.md)). Shared helpers are in `ArrangementInternal.h`; the clip-edit arithmetic is the pure header `model/ClipGeometry.h`, unit-tested without a `Session`.

## How it stays responsive and correct

- **Preview, then one commit.** A drag edits a local `preview`, and the model's command and its undo transaction run once, on release: `Session::editClip` for a trim and, since 2026-10-23, `Session::moveClipRegion` for a move, because a header press carries the whole selection ([Region editing](region-editing.md#moving-a-selection)). During a move `original` and `preview` are the grabbed piece, the part of the grabbed clip the selection carries or the whole clip, and the selection follows it by the same distance (`displayedTimeSelection`); `snappedClipMoveStart` skips the clips the selection carries. Mid-drag, a change notification only repaints. Arrow nudges go through `moveClipRegion` too and are taken whenever the selection is a range, otherwise reaching the window (commit `d20faa4`); until 2026-10-23 a move and a nudge were `Session::moveClips` over the selected clips (commit `b5d17e0`), whose loop is now the shared `moveClipsInEdit`.
- **Clip views are a cache, kept per track** (2026-10-23). `sync()` rebuilds the `ClipView` list on each change notification, never per paint, but reads a track's clips again (`rebuildTrackClips`) only when its `Session::trackRevision` has moved since or the track is new. A `TrackClipCache` per track id holds the revision, the views, the files their waveforms draw and the sync pass that last kept it; a sync copies the views into `clips` with the track's current index, drops the caches of tracks that are gone, and `editWillChange` clears them all. At 135 tracks and 103,072 notes a sync fell from 11.9 to 2.7 ms ([The 120-track stress audit](stress-audit-2026-10.md)). A command that changes what a clip shows has to mark its track, or the cache keeps the old view ([What a change costs the interface](ui-cost-of-a-change.md)). Waveforms share one thumbnail per source file. A `Session` call announces synchronously, so the list can be rebuilt before the call returns: copy a `ClipView` before calling `Session` if you read it afterwards (commit `2df76f8`). The sync also reads each track's facts once (`TrackFacts`), and the painter skips whatever a repaint does not reach ([What a change costs the interface](ui-cost-of-a-change.md)).
- **A MIDI clip's notes are kept in the sequence's own seconds.** `ClipView::midiNotes` is a `shared_ptr<const std::vector<MidiNoteView>>`, shared with the track's cache, and the painter draws each note at `xFor(note.start + origin)` with `origin = position.start - position.offset`, the origin of the window the clip is onto its sequence. A clip moved or trimmed, and its preview mid-drag, converts nothing, and the old `noteShift` arithmetic is gone. Since commit `4a6a17b` (2026-10-27), `rebuildTrackClips` reads `getSequenceLooped()` rather than `getSequence()`, so a looped clip that has been dragged longer draws its repeats from the same origin ([Clip Loop switch](clip-loop.md)). Since commit `81a898b` such a clip is also notched where each pass begins ([Rules worth knowing](#rules-worth-knowing)). `clipNameWidths`, the measured width of each name, is kept across syncs, cleared past 8,192 entries and with the document.
- **Ids, not pointers.** The selection is a `TimeSelection` rectangle (start, end, first and last track) plus `selected`, the primary clip's `te::EditItemID`; nothing keeps a `te::Clip*`, because one dies on undo or project replacement ([Hold ids, not pointers](ids-not-pointers.md)).
- **`rows` is the vertical authority.** Revealed automation lanes stack extra rows under a track, and collapsed group members and the lane rows of a track whose automation is folded get zero-height rows, so `lane(track)` looks the track up in `rows` rather than multiplying. A return's rows are `pinned`: laid out first and measured from `returnsTop()`, not scrolled with the lanes ([Return tracks and sends](return-tracks.md#in-the-arrangement)).

## Rules worth knowing

- **A clip fills its row** (commit `81a898b`, 2026-10-27). The user's request: "can we make our clips fit into the entire cell instead of leaving empty space". `boundsOf` places every clip in `clipBandOf(row)`: the row from its top down to the 2 px rule under it (`row.withTrimmedBottom(trackDividerThickness)`, never leaving less than 1 px). This applies on every lane, return and folded row. Until then a clip stood 5 px in from the row's top and bottom (`row.getY() + 5`, height − 10). Hit testing follows the box, so a press anywhere in the row's height over a clip lands on the clip. Anything that measures part of a clip has to start from `clipBandOf`, not repeat an inset. The automation curve on a track's own row does so ([Track automation](automation.md#the-automation-chooser)), and so does `AutomationLanes.inc`. `ClipLoop.inc` checks that a clip's box runs from its lane's top to the lane's bottom less `trackDividerThickness`.
- **A looped clip is notched at each pass** (commit `81a898b`), as Live notches one. The user asked for "a tiny ruler to differentiate looped clips from normal extended midi clips".
  - `ClipView::loopSeconds` is one pass of a looped MIDI clip's loop in seconds, and 0 for a clip that plays once. `rebuildTrackClips` sets it from `getLoopLengthBeats` through `session.beatsToSeconds`.
  - `paintClipPiece` draws a tick 2 px wide and 6 px tall down from the box's top wherever a pass begins. It uses `label.darker(0.75f)`, the colour of the rule under the name strip.
  - Passes run from the window's origin, `start - offset`, because the offset counts into the loop. A left-trimmed clip is therefore notched where its passes really begin, and the clip's own start edge gets no notch.
  - The notches are worked out per displayed piece, so a carried selection's pieces are notched too.
  - Only the visible part of the dirty range is walked, and no notch is drawn when one pass is narrower than 4 px on screen.
  - `ClipLoop.inc` paints the arrangement on a software image. It checks that the column at the second pass differs from a column 12 px along the top strip, and that it does not once looping is off. `RHINO_CLIP_LOOP_SNAPSHOT=<png>` writes the looped clip's lane zoomed to three bars, at 2x ([Seeing the UI without taking the screen](headless-ui-snapshots.md)).
- **A clip is two rows.** The top strip (`clipHeaderHeight`: half the clip, clamped to 8-22 px) selects the clip's own span or, when the selection already touches the clip, carries the whole selection ([Pointer and selection rules](pointer-and-selection.md)); the body behaves like empty lane, so a press there sets the selection line and a drag sweeps a region, except on a row whose card's chooser shows an automation lane, where the body edits that curve ([Track automation](automation.md#the-automation-chooser)). Edges trim at any height, and a trim edits that one clip.
- **A carried selection is drawn in pieces, and marked where it cannot land** (2026-10-23). `displayedPieces` (`ArrangementGeometry.cpp`) yields up to three pieces per clip - the remainder left before the region, the carried piece at its previewed place on its previewed track, the remainder after - from `carriedCut`, which is the model's own `cutClipByRegion` (`model/ClipGeometry.h`); `paintClipPiece` outlines a piece as selected when it is carried or the selection holds the whole clip. `previewRowRefused(row)` predicts from the sync's `TrackFacts` (bus, midi) and `ClipView::midi` (set in `ArrangementSync.cpp`) whether a row's material could land on its destination row, and `paintTimeSelection` draws Live's circle-with-slash (`palette::recordAccent`, 16 px) at the left edge of the carried rectangle on each refused row; the model is the authority on release, and its refusal goes to the status line. `factsFor(track)` answers a default `TrackFacts` for a track past the end, which the preview and the clip painter rely on for a lane the drop would make. The sync no longer derives the region from a set of selected clips, but the selection follows the clip it is exactly: `sync()` asks `isExactly` of the primary clip on the old list before rebuilding, then sets the rectangle to the clip's new span, or clears it when the clip is gone; any other rectangle only has its rows clamped and stays where it was put. A primary clip that has left the list is dropped either way, announcing `clipSelected({})` ([Region editing](region-editing.md#one-selection)).
- **The selection line is where playback starts.** `moveTransportToSelectionStart` always calls `Session::setPlaybackStart`, but moves the transport only while stopped and not counting in.
- **Two snapping rules, and clip edges join them.** Clip edges round to the nearest line (`snapped`); a pointer position takes the cell it is inside (`snappedDown`/`snappedUp`). The grid is adaptive by default (`ArrangementGrid.h`); see [Zoom and the adaptive grid](#zoom-and-the-adaptive-grid). Since commit `c1e61d1` (2026-10-26; the user's screenshot: a sweep meant to start at an off-grid clip began at the grid line a few px before it), the edges of clips snap too, all in `ArrangementGeometry.cpp`:
  - *A sweep and the insert point* (`beginRegionGesture`, `dragRegionGesture`) call `snappedDown`/`snappedUp(seconds, bypass, firstTrack, lastTrack)`, which read the clips on the rows the region spans, `regionAnchorTrack` through the pointer's. In `snappedDown` an edge at or behind the pointer cuts its grid cell (the line is the larger of the floor and that edge), and the nearest edge just ahead, within `edgeSnapPixels` (14 px, as seconds `edgeSnapPixels / lane(0).getWidth() * viewSpan`), wins if it is nearer the pointer than that line. `snappedUp` is the mirror, so overshooting a clip's end snaps back to it. The on-edge allowance is the grid's, 1e-3 of a cell (`snapUnitSeconds`). `edgeSnapPixels` is one constant at the top of the file, shared with `snappedClipMoveStart`.
  - *A trim* (`Arrangement::mouseDrag`) calls `snapped(seconds, bypass, track, except)`: the nearest of the grid lines and the edges of the other clips on the trimmed clip's own track (`selected`, the trimmed clip, excluded), with no reach limit, since the nearest grid line is never more than half a cell away.
  - A move keeps its 14 px magnet to clip edges on the target track (`snappedClipMoveStart`); a new clip, the ruler's insert point, the loop brace, automation points and nudges stay grid only. Alt bypasses all of it, and with the grid off nothing snaps, clip edges included.
  - *Why both parts.* The 7 px just inside a clip's start is its trim handle (`clipZoneAt`: `min(7, width / 4)`, at any height), so a press meant for the start either grabs the trim or falls just short, in the cell before. The first version, edges as cell cuts alone, took the grid line before the clip there, which is what the screenshot showed, and failed its own test. The reach ahead lands that press on the start. The cut lands any press past the handle and inside the cell on the start too, even beyond 14 px. Only an edge nearer than the line the pointer would otherwise take wins, so a grid line beside an edge stays reachable from the line to the midpoint between them; a plain 14 px magnet would have made it unreachable. Only the spanned rows count, so a clip on another track pulls nothing, and a trim reads one track so that a 120-track song does not fill every cell with edges.
  - `RegionClipboard.inc` pins it at a fixed 1/16 at 120 BPM, zoomed to 5.5-8.5 s (about 30 px a sixteenth) round an audio clip at 6.08 s: 4 px short of its start and 10 px inside both give 6.08, 3 px past the 6.0 line gives 6.0, a sweep overshooting the end by 4 px selects exactly the clip, the same sweep on an empty lane gives the grid alone, and Alt the raw pointer. The trim check uses MIDI clips because an audio clip cannot be pulled past the end of its file.
- **A new clip fills its grid cell** (commit `3a0933c`). A double-click on an empty lane (`ArrangementGestures.cpp`) and Ctrl+A at the playhead (`Arrangement.cpp`) start it at `snappedDown`, the cell the pointer or playhead is in, and pass `newClipBeats()`: the resolved cell (`resolvedGridBeats()`, triplet included), or 0 with the grid off. `Session::createClip` makes it that long but never under a bar, and ends it at a clip ahead ([Clips never overlap](clip-placement.md)): one bar at the opening zoom, two on a two-bar grid, up to eight. Before, it was always one bar, half a cell on a two-bar grid, and started at `snapped`, so a click in the right half of a long cell made the clip in the next one. `NoteEditor.inc` pins the lengths and the start.
- **The bar ruler labels counts too.** Bar numbers step by powers of two bars, 44 px apart (`barLabelStep`, `barNumberMinimumPixels`). Once they are one bar apart, `paintBarNumbers` also labels counts as `bar.count` with short marks between, like Live. A count is the signature's denominator note (`timeSignature().numerator` per bar, so 6/8 reads 1.2 to 1.6 in eighths), not the engine's quarter-note beat (`beatsPerBar()`). Every span comes from one chain in `ArrangementGrid.h`, `finerRulerSpan`, where each span divides the previous: 4/4 runs 4, 2, 1, 1/2, 1/4; 6/8 runs 6, 3, 1; 7/8 runs 7, 1. That chain is what makes every mark land on a division of the labels either side. `rulerLabelSpan` keeps labels 44 px apart and never goes finer than a count. `rulerTickSpan` keeps marks 10 px apart and stops at a quarter count. The ruler ignores the snap grid. The time row at the foot no longer follows the bar numbers: it reads round times, then the grid ([The time row](#the-time-row)). Unit-tested in `tests/Arrangement/ClipGeometryTest.cpp`.
- **`Focus`** records what the last click selected (`none`, `region`, `track` or `automation`; `Focus::clip` went with the one-selection change of 2026-10-23, a clicked clip being a region), and Delete, Ctrl+B, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+D dispatch on it: on `Focus::track` they act on every selected card ([Track clipboard](track-clipboard.md)), otherwise on the region. Ctrl+V follows whichever clipboard was filled last.
- **A muted clip is drawn grey** (since 2026-10-14): `ClipView::muted`, a desaturated body, grey waveform and dim notes in `ArrangementPainter.cpp`. The clip menu's ticked *Mute* (`muteSelected`, `Session::setClipsMuted`) mutes either kind of clip as one undo step; before, a muted audio clip looked like any other and a MIDI clip could not be muted at all.
- **The clip menu (`showClipMenu`) acts on the selection, except** *Copy to the track's DJ clips*, Split, the warp items and, since 2026-10-27, *Slice to New MIDI Track*, which act on the clip it was opened on. The slicing submenu (ids 40-45) is enabled only on an audio clip; it is one clip because Live slices one ([Slice to New MIDI Track](slice-to-new-midi-track.md)).
- **Anything painted on the lanes clips at `headerWidth`** (commit `137ccb5`, 2026-10-24). `xFor` gives true positions, so a clip starting before `viewStart` has a `box` (`boundsOf`) that runs left under the track cards, and `paint()` draws the cards before the clips, so nothing covers a spill. `paintClipPiece` (`ArrangementClipPainter.cpp`) clipped at x 0, the panel's edge, a rule older than the painter's split in `cff657d`: scrolled past a clip's start, its body fill, 4 px top strip and outline were painted across the card's controls and name columns in the clip's colour (the name, notes and waveform were already held to `visible`). Its clip region and `visible` now both run from `headerWidth` to `getWidth() - 14`, as the automation rows, the recording band and `paintTimeSelection` already did; only the row dividers span from x 0 across the cards, deliberately. `Rendering.inc` paints the panel with the view inside the pattern clip and with the clip out of sight, and requires the card column across lane 0 to match pixel for pixel.
- **Empty lanes and empty clips are blank** (commit `c462431`, 2026-10-24, at the user's request). Two placeholder texts went: the empty-lane hint `Arrangement::paint` drew on the first empty, unhidden, non-bus track without an instrument ("Double-click to add a clip, or drop an instrument here" on a MIDI lane, "Drop audio here" on an audio lane), and the "Edit notes below" `paintClipPiece` drew in a MIDI clip with no notes. `TrackFacts::holdsInstrument`, read only by the hint, went with it, so a sync no longer asks `Session::trackHasInstrument`.
- **Autoscroll runs on the frame clock** (`VBlankAttachment`), because a pointer held still past the edge sends no drag events. So do the level meters and, since `fd03386`, automated fader and pan bars: each frame runs `autoScrollDrag`, `updateMeters`, `followMixerAutomation` (only while showing) and `updatePlayhead`, in that order, so the playhead's immediate `UpdateWindow` handoff also collects the meters' damage ([Playhead rendering](playhead.md)).
- **A card reads meter, controls, name** (commits `14c9c77` and `83135f7`, 2026-10-15). The header is `headerWidth` 272 px: a `LevelMeter` at `cardMeterInset` (9) plus the group indent (`groupIndent` 13 px for a group, `nestedGroupIndent` 4 px more per further level since groups nest; [Track groups](track-groups.md#nesting)), 8 px of room, the controls column (`cardControlsWidth` 148, its controls from `cardControlLeft` 24 and 116 px wide), a 4 px divider, and the name column, 120 px on the track's colour. Three buttons and their gaps (3 × 36 + 2 × 4) span exactly the two faders and theirs (2 × 56 + 4), so the buttons sit squarely over the faders. While the song has return tracks (since commit `e6473f5`), a send column stands at `cardControlLeft` plus the indent, between the meter and the buttons. It is `sendColumnWidth()` wide: 24 px (`SendKnob::size` + `cardControlGap`), or 0 with no returns. It pushes every card's controls right to `cardControlsLeft()`, the returns' and the main row's included, so the columns stay aligned. The name column, its divider and the fold arrow then start at `cardControlsRight()`, so with returns the name column is 24 px narrower, since `headerWidth` does not change. Everything placed from these positions shifts with the column. The third line, from `inputLaneHeight` (72 px), holds the input chooser in the first fader column (`cardControlWidth`, 56 px; it spanned both until 2026-10-25) and the automation chooser in the second, on every track including a bus, which has no input chooser; the monitor control under them still spans both ([Track automation](automation.md#the-automation-chooser)). Both choosers are a `ChooserButton` (`ArrangementSync.cpp`): at one column the stock `TextButton` squeezed and shrank most input names off whole pixels, which the user saw as small, pixelated text, so it sets the label in the font the stock button starts from (`getTextButtonFont`) through `drawSnappedText`, elided with an ellipsis and nothing else (the user asked to keep it light), and paints the menu's "v" itself at the right edge so a cut name keeps it (commit `7261d28`). Labels no longer carry a "  v" suffix ([Colours and typography](colours-and-typography.md#rhino-type-and-icons)). The toolbar's A button, a global automation edit mode, went at the same time (commit `92b4258`). Since `5632644` the main row has a card too: the meter, volume at `cardControlsLeft()` with pan beside it at a track's fader widths, the divider, and a name column in Main's colour with "Main" in bold. The MAIN label it had in the controls column is gone, and the rule between card and lane runs through the main row. Right-clicking the card opens `showMainMenu`, the track swatches and "No colour" ([The main track](main-track.md)). `updateMeters` is the single reader of `Session::takeOutputLevels`, skips while the arrangement is hidden, and drops the bars to empty on its return ([Track level meters](level-meters.md)).
- **A selected track's whole row is lit** (commit `83135f7`, 2026-10-15). `paintTrackCards` lays `palette::selectedRowWash` over the card and over the lane's ground across the arrangement for every selected track (`isTrackSelected`, the whole multi-selection), under the bar bands, grid lines and clips. It replaces the 3 px `palette::selection` strip at the card's left edge, so nothing now marks which of several selected cards is the working track; the Device View still follows it. Selected, the main row takes the same wash on its card and lane, instead of `palette::hover` and the strip. `TrackManagement.inc` checks it by painting the arrangement twice, row 0 selected and then row 1, and the main row the same way ([Writing Rhino tests](writing-rhino-tests.md)).
- **Every card has a fold arrow, before its name** (commit `14c9c77`). `disclosureBounds(track)` puts it at the start of the name column on the name's line; the name, and its rename editor, start `disclosureGap` after it and end 6 px short of the header edge (`trackNameBounds`). `paintDisclosure` draws it in the name's own colour, pointing at what a click reveals; `beginDisclosureGesture` takes the click. On a bus it collapses or expands the group's members and never folds the bus row ([Track groups](track-groups.md)). On any other card, members included, it folds the row to `minimumLaneHeight` (32 px: the name and the button line) as Live's fold button does, and unfolds it to the height it had: `Session::setTrackFolded` writes `rhinoTrackFolded` (present only while folded, a view setting with no undo, `ModelChange::none`), `laneHeightFor` answers the minimum while it is set and leaves `rhinoLaneHeight` alone, and `setTrackLaneHeight` clears it, so dragging a folded card's bottom edge unfolds it. `TrackFacts::folded` gives the arrow its direction once per sync. A card with automation lanes on rows of their own also carries a second arrow, in its bottom-right corner, that folds those rows away (`TrackFacts::automationFolded`; commit `99b4e24`, 2026-10-25); `mouseDown` offers both arrows before card select or carry, and the name stops short of the second where they share a line ([Track automation](automation.md#folding-the-lanes-under-a-track)).
- **Waveforms already fill their height.** `paintWaveformLanes` draws JUCE's `AudioThumbnail` (peaks stored as int8 × 127, drawn at zoom × height / 256) at zoom 1.45 (`ArrangementPainter.cpp`), so a peak of about -3.2 dBFS reaches the lane edge and anything louder is clipped at it. In a default 96 px row the clip box is 94 px since commit `81a898b` (86 px before), its name strip and padding take 36, and the 58 px left (50 before) is split between two channel lanes. A waveform that looks small is quiet material: a recording reported as loud (2026-10-15) peaked at -4.4 dBFS but 90% of its pixel columns sat below -12.6 dBFS. Measure the file before changing the drawing ([Measure sound, don't read the DSP](measure-sound-dont-read-dsp.md)).
- **It reports, the shell decides.** `clipSelected`, `clipOpened`, `clipSpanSwept`, `trackSelected` and `trackFocused` are callbacks; which panel opens is the [app shell](app-shell.md)'s call. `clipSpanSwept(clip, start, end)` (2026-10-24) fires when a finished sweep has named a primary clip, which the shell passes to the note editor ([Region editing](region-editing.md#one-selection)). The shell also reads `selectedClipID()` on every session change, not only on `clipSelected`, so changing `selected` mid-gesture is visible to it even unannounced.
- **Cards.** A card's bottom edge resizes its row and its body carries the track through the stack (`Session::moveTrack`), both previewed until release. Row height (`rhinoLaneHeight`, 0 means fit the panel) is a view setting written without undo; colour and name are undoable. Renaming puts one `TextEditor` over the painted name. A track from the `+` menu or Ctrl+T lands under the selected card and becomes the selection (`insertTrack`, see [Track kinds: audio and MIDI](track-kinds.md)). The lanes scroll `laneTailHeight` (40 px, `Arrangement.h`) past the last lane, as Live's do (commit `100bf20`, at the user's request): `updateScroll` and the vertical-wheel test in `mouseWheelMove` both total `rowsHeight + laneTailHeight`. Without it, scrolled to the end, the last lane's bottom edge lay on `returnsTop()`, where `cardResizeEdgeAt` ignores a lane edge (`edge > pinnedTop - grab`, because the first return's top-edge handle is on the same pixels), so once the stack outgrew the panel the last track could not be resized. The bare strip under the last track is also where a drop below `lastLaneBottom()` makes a new lane. `ArrangementPan.inc` checks the gap and that the edge answers.
- **Sends and return cards** (commit `b7bd73c`, 2026-10-25; [Return tracks and sends](return-tracks.md)). Since `5632644` the returns are drawn pinned between the lanes and the main row: their controls sit in a second container, `returnHeaders`; a pinned row resizes from its top edge; a carried card stays among its own kind; and the cards, rules and automation rows are painted in two clipped passes. The details are in [Return tracks and sends](return-tracks.md#in-the-arrangement). `syncSendKnobs` (`ArrangementSync.cpp`) keeps one `SendKnob` per return on every card that is not a return, in `sends`. Since `e6473f5`, `resized` stacks them in the send column, one return under another from the button line down, `SendKnob::size + cardControlGap` apart. Before that they sat under the name and wrapped. At the user's wish the card never grows to fit them. A knob shows only while `cardControlsTop` plus its offset and its size fits the row's height less the divider, so it stays hidden until the row is dragged taller, and a one-line row (32 px) shows only A. Each drag is a bracketed send gesture that writes its level to the status line. A return's card paints its letter in place of the row number (`TrackFacts::returnLetter`) and shows no arm, input or monitor control. The `+` menu's *Return Track* and Ctrl+Alt+T call `insertReturnTrack`, which selects the new return. The returns are past the last lane. A region stops at the last lane, a carried selection's first row stops at `firstReturnTrack()`, and `previewRowRefused` marks no refusal on a return's row, because the model makes lanes above the returns. The carried clip is previewed in the slot where that lane will appear (`laneOrSlot`).

## Zoom and the adaptive grid

Since 2026-10-23, modelled on Ableton Live but not identical to it (`Arrangement.h`, `Arrangement::zoom`, `ArrangementGrid.h`).

- **It opens on two minutes.** `viewSpan` is seconds across the lane width and starts at `defaultViewSeconds` (120; it was 8). At 120 BPM the adaptive grid reads that in one-bar cells on any lane about 1,360-2,700 px wide (a 1920 or 2560 screen with the browser open). Zoom-out stops at max(2 × `defaultViewSeconds`, 2 × `songEnd`); the old cap, max(60 s, 2 × song), would clamp the first zoom-out from the opening view back to one minute.
- **The view scrolls 32 bars past the song's end** (commit `060e69b`, at the user's request). `updateScroll` sets the horizontal extent to `songEnd + timelineTailBars * bar + viewSpan` (`timelineTailBars` 32, `Arrangement.h`), so the view's left edge reaches 32 bars past the end at any zoom, in an empty song too. The scrollbar, the middle-drag pan and the clip-drag autoscroll all clamp through it. The bar follows the time signature (`beatsPerBar()`). Before, the extent was max(8 s, song + one 4/4 bar, `viewSpan`): zoomed in that left a screen or less to scroll or drag a clip into, and an empty song stopped at 8 s. `ArrangementPan.inc` checks the end at a one-bar and a 240 s view.
- **Zoom-in stops at 1/16384.** The floor is lane width ÷ `deepestZoomPixelsPerBeat` × seconds per beat (it was a fixed 0.25 s). `deepestZoomPixelsPerBeat` is the medium target over a 1/16384, 32 × 4096 = 131,072 px per beat, held per beat so the deepest view shows 1/16384 at every tempo: about 262 px/ms at 120 BPM. That is shallower in time than Live: a screenshot at Live's maximum zoom shows a single whole-millisecond reading across about 975 px, so Live reaches at least about 650 px/ms. Live's grid, though, appears to stop at 1/8192: it draws 1/8192 lines 14 px apart at one zoom and still 28 px apart at a deeper one, where it would otherwise have halved them.
- **The adaptive grid picks the division nearest its target.** `adaptiveGridDivision` takes the finest division whose lines are at least target ÷ √2 apart (22.6 px at medium), which for divisions an octave apart is the nearest one. The old rule, finest that clears the whole target, often read one division coarser: over two minutes at 120 BPM it needed a 1,920 px lane for one-bar cells where the new rule needs about 1,360, and at the zoom of one Live screenshot it chose 1/4096 lines 56 px apart where Live drew 1/8192 lines 28 px apart. Live draws denser still: one-bar cells 20 px apart (two minutes on a ~1,157 px lane), 1/8192 lines 14 px apart. The √2 rule was kept because at Live's density two minutes across the user's ~2,030 px lane would read in half bars, and the user asked for one bar there. The five targets (72/48/32/20/12 px) are unchanged.
- **Divisions run past 1/64**, `note128` to `note16384`. The Fixed menu lists only the first `fixedGridDivisionCount` (10, through 1/64), as Live's stops early; the finer ones come from the adaptive grid and Ctrl+1, since `narrowerGridDivision` walks the whole chain.
- **A grid too fine to draw is drawn coarser; snapping keeps it.** `drawnGridBeats` steps a fixed or off-mode grid whose lines fall under `gridLineMinimumPixels` (4 px) out to the counts, then the bars, and since commit `cf4e959` draws bars still that close every other bar, then every fourth, and so on, as a fixed one-bar grid across a long song needs. Drawn line for line, 1/64 across two minutes is about 3,840 lines, past the painter's 2,000-line cap, which left the right of the view bare.
- **The adaptive grid still reaches eight bars.** The user briefly asked to cap it at one bar, then kept multi-bar cells because songs of eight minutes and more need them (2026-10-23).

Covered by `tests/Arrangement/ClipGeometryTest.cpp` (the nearest rule, the deepest zoom reaching `note16384`, `drawnGridBeats` and its bar thinning) and the scenario `BrowserDrops.inc` (one-bar opening at lane widths 1673 and 2313, zoom-out widening it, nothing past 1/16384).

**Open at deep zoom** (as of 2026-10-23):

- The bar ruler still stops, labels at counts and marks at a quarter count (`rulerFinestSpan`), so at deep zoom the top ruler is empty; Live's keeps fine ticks there. The [time row](#the-time-row) reads at every zoom.
- Waveforms are `AudioThumbnail` peaks, blocky rather than single samples below thumbnail resolution.
- `xFor` returns float: a clip starting minutes before a deep view, tens of millions of pixels off-screen, loses a few pixels of edge precision, and past about 2^31 px (about 2.3 hours at 120 BPM at the deepest zoom) an integer conversion would overflow.
- The 92 px toolbar grid button squeezes "Grid 1/16384" and its arrow to about 86% width (79% with T), inside JUCE's 0.7 floor.

## The time row

The strip at the foot, above the horizontal scrollbar, reads wall-clock time: `paintTimeRuler` in `ArrangementPainter.cpp`, its steps in `ArrangementGrid.h`, its text in `ui/shared/WallClock.h`. Since commit `61822a1` (2026-10-23) it reads round times, as Live's does, and readings on the grid where round times run out, which the user accepted. Before, it stepped by whole bars so that every time stood under a bar number (commit `baf0d38`); at 120 BPM that jumped 2 s at a time at a 1/32 grid and printed nothing at 1/512 or deeper, where no bar starts inside the view. `timeTicksPerLabel` is gone; `barLabelStep` and `firstLabelledBar` now serve the bar numbers alone.

- **Round times.** `roundTimeSteps`, in microseconds: 100, 200 and 500 µs (marks only), 1, 2 and 5 × 10^k ms, 1, 2, 5, 10, 15 and 30 s, and 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 min. `roundTimeLabelStep(pixelsPerSecond)` takes the finest of 1 ms or more that leaves `timeLabelMinimumPixels` (88 px) between readings; `roundTimeTickStep` marks the gap with the finest round step that cuts it into at most `timeTickDivisions` (8), at least `timeTickMinimumPixels` (20 px) apart. Whole seconds and minutes print `mm:ss` (00:10), finer steps `mm:ss:mmm`. On a 2,027 px lane at 120 BPM: two minutes read 00:00, 00:10 ... 01:50, a 1/32 view every 200 ms, a 1/512 view every 20 ms.
- **Then the grid.** `timeRowReadsGrid(gridReadingSeconds)` hands the row to the grid's own divisions once they read at least twice as often as whole milliseconds (0.5 ms apart or less); any sooner would trade round readings for ones barely closer. `timeLabelSpan(pixelsPerCount, countsPerBar)` walks the bar ruler's chain (`finerRulerSpan`) on below the counts, floored only at 1e-6 counts, and `timeTickSpan` marks down the same chain, so every reading sits on a grid line. Readings carry `clockDecimalsForStep` decimals of a millisecond (none at 100 ms apart or more, one more per tenfold closer, at most 3, so three in practice), printed by `formatClock(seconds, withMilliseconds, millisecondDecimals)`. The deepest zoom at 120 BPM reads 00:01:300.293, 00:01:300.781 ..., 0.488 ms apart. The hand-over comes at about 180 px/ms at 120 BPM; at 40 BPM never, the deepest zoom still reading round milliseconds.
- **Marks count from the start of the song** (time 0, or count 0 on the grid), not from the view's edge, so the labelled ones do not shuffle on scroll. The paint loop caps the marks it paints (4096), not the mark's index: inside a deeply zoomed bar the first visible index is far past 4096.
- **Tests.** `ClipGeometryTest.cpp` sweeps the round steps, the grid chain, the hand-over, the decimals and `formatClock`, and at 120 BPM in 4/4 keeps readings at least 88 px and under 2.5 × 88 px apart from hours down to the deepest zoom, a 1/32 view reading every 500 ms or closer and a 1/512 view every 20 ms or closer. `BrowserDrops.inc` paints the row at four zooms and requires text between bars at 1/512 and at the deepest zoom; with `RHINO_TIME_ROW_SNAPSHOT=<path.png>` set, the arrangement workflow under ctest writes the four rows stacked (two minutes, 1/32, 1/512, deepest; [Seeing the UI without taking the screen](headless-ui-snapshots.md)).

## Clips that leave the panel

A clip dragged up out of the panel by more than the band where the lanes scroll (24 px above the top) stops being a move (one clip leaves, the primary selection, whatever else is selected): `Arrangement::mouseDrag` cancels the gesture, so the clip stays put, and starts a window-wide drag carrying `rhino-clip:<id>` (`ClipDrag.h`) with a small tile for a picture. The DJ toggle in the control bar shows the consoles while that drag hovers it, and a console's clips grid takes a copy. The lanes take the reverse: a console's cell dropped on a lane is copied there at the drop point, held to the lane's kind (`ArrangementDrops.cpp`, `rhino-slot:`). See [DJ view and the booth](dj-view.md).
