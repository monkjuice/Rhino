# DJ deck engine

The audio-thread half of [DJ view and the booth](dj-view.md), in `native/src/core`: `DjDeck.h` with `DjDeck.cpp` (controls, queries, material taken up and let go of, `jumpTo`), `DjDeckTransport.cpp`, `DjDeckLoops.cpp` and `DjDeckRender.cpp` (the plan and the read); `DjEngine.h` with `DjEngine.cpp` (commands, slots, the hand-over), `DjEngineClock.cpp` (master, sync, landings) and `DjEngineMix.cpp` (the block, the sequencer); `DjTrackPort.*` (the port into a track, the block delay); `DjReleaseQueue.*` and `DjLinearRamp.h`. Described as of step 2 of the live-chain work (2026-10-11) and the track port that replaced the bounces (2026-10-12); the tests are `tests/DjTest.cpp`.

## A block

`DjEngine::process` applies every queued command first, for every slot, material included, so the block sees one consistent material and position before anything is decided. Then, per chunk of at most the prepared block: `chooseMaster`, `setRates`, `landPendings`, and each active slot planned, read, its notes sequenced, sent into its track's port and taken back from it when the port is bound or delayed a block when it is not, and on through its strip. What the block let go of goes back to the message thread last, after its final read.

## The plan and the read

`DjDeck::plan(frames, deviceRate)` holds every rule of the transport - starts and stops with their 2 ms fades, the rate smoothed over 30 ms or wound by the brake, the loop, the end of the material, the landings the engine timed - and writes per frame where the material is read (`planPos`), how loud (`planGain`), what the deck fades from (`planFrom`, `planFromAt`, `planFromWeight`) and why a frame starts a new stretch (`planMark`: started, landed, jumped, wrapped). `DjDeck::read` only reads, with four-point Hermite, both channels from one index and fraction; material with no audio, a MIDI cell's (`DjTrack::silentFrames` long, which `length()` reads), reads as silence, and its notes leave through the sequencer below. Plain playback is the old one-piece player's to the bit: `DjDeckTest::plainPlaybackUnchanged` keeps that player in the test and compares every sample, on a ramp read at another rate and on noise, at the material's tempo and at +6 per cent.

## Loops and the end

- Loop points stand in `[0, length]`: a whole-track loop, which every fresh cell lands with, ends at the length. Until step 2 the end test fired at `length - 1`, before the loop's end, so a bounce played once and stopped.
- The wrap is a modulo, `lStart + fmod(pos - lStart, length)`, and the loop is read once a block. The old wrap took one loop length a frame, so a jump past a short loop scanned back through the track for tens of milliseconds and stayed trapped.
- Any landing outside an active loop - a seek, a hot cue, the cue's return, a beat jump the loop cannot follow - leaves it, through `DjDeck::jumpTo`, as a CDJ's does. A beat jump carries the loop with it when the loop fits in the material.
- Sync's alignment (`DjDeck::alignPhase`) is not a landing the DJ asked for and never leaves a loop. A deck playing in its loop lands a span along when that lies inside the loop, so bars still line up, and otherwise where the loop would carry it, a loop's length round, which keeps the beat's phase when the loop is whole beats; outside its loop it moves through `moveTo`, which leaves the loop alone. Until the review of step 2 it went through `jumpTo`, and a target up to half a bar away dropped the loop.
- The end of the material, tested after the wrap, stops the deck through the stop's fade, reading the end sample held, rather than cutting.
- A swap by beat onto a deck looping its whole material loops the new material whole, and a point past the new one's end comes round by its length: a one-bar cell swapped in at beat 21.5 of an eight-bar song comes in at beat 1.5.

## The declick

A jump, a wrap, a change of material and an eject crossfade over 5 ms (`DjDeck::declickSeconds`) from the old material read on from where it was at the rate it had, its weight a `DjLinearRamp` counted in frames that reaches exactly 0 on its last frame, whatever the block size. A wrap fades out what lies past the loop's end while its start fades in, over the loop's own length when that is shorter, a length counted in floating point before it becomes an int: at a crawl a whole track's loop is more frames than an int holds, and the cast came out a one-frame fade on MSVC. `DjDeckTest::declick` holds a sine to 1.5 times its own steepest step and a constant level to no movement at all, through a jump and a wrap, in blocks of 512 and 64; `crawlingWrap` holds the crawl.

A discontinuity inside another's fade nests: the running fade goes under the new one (`DjDeck::under`, read in `read` as a second crossfade) and fades out of what the new one fades from, so the new one fades from what was heard. A fade not a frame of which has been read is kept instead, as long as the longer of the two, since what the deck was about to play in its place was never heard: that is how any number of jumps queued for one block crossfade as one. Two levels deep; a third discontinuity inside both lets the oldest go. Until the review of step 2 a new fade replaced the running one, and a quantised jump landing on the frame a loop wrapped - every press, for a bounce at the device's rate - cut the wrap's fade and stepped by the whole seam. `DjDeckTest::fadesUnderFades` lands a jump on a wrap's frame, the frame before, and one, five and 120 frames into its fade, two jumps fifty frames apart and three in one block.

## Quantised landings

A pending start or hot-cue jump keeps its target exactly and lands when the master's phase within the boundary (a beat, a bar of the master's own `beatsPerBar`, or four) reaches the target's phase on the deck's own grid, at that frame inside the block (`startAt`, `jumpAt`); a hair past, 0.002 beats, counts as on it. A cue on a bar's second beat lands as the master reaches a bar's second beat. The master times its own jumps against its own grid, so its phase, and every synced deck's, runs on through them. With quantise off, no master, or a master going nowhere forwards, it lands at once. A beat jump moves exactly so many beats from where the deck stands. Before step 2 a start or a cue was snapped to the deck's nearest bar and landed on the master's next bar line, a jump landed at the block's start, and the master's own jumps landed at once.

`DjEngine::landingFrame` reads the master on as its plan will, stretch by stretch through its loop's wraps, rather than in a straight line it may never travel. A loop that never passes the target's phase within the span - a beat looped off the bar line while a cue waits for the bar line - is timed by the beat's phase instead, and one that never passes even that, by its wrap: boundaries the master does reach. A crossing in the last half frame before a wrap lands on the wrapped frame only when the wrap keeps the phase, a loop of whole spans. A deck that follows the master counts the master's beats folded as its tempo and lock fold them, so 87 BPM synced to 174 lands on the beat it is locked to. Until the review of step 2 the projection was straight and unfolded: a master looping a beat off the bar line never landed its own bar-line hot cue, nor another deck's start against it, and a folded deck landed half a beat off its lock, which the 2 per cent correction took some 17 s to close (`checkLandingsInLoops`, `checkSyncFollowsTempo`).

## Sync

Each deck publishes its tempo (`tempoRate`: fader and push, a synced deck's master's tempo, or a held tempo) apart from its platter's speed (`playbackRate`), and sync follows the master's tempo, never its platter, so a braked, scratched or reversed master drags nobody. The ratio is folded by an octave either way to the nearest one, so 87 BPM synced to 174 plays at its own tempo, on every other beat. Phase correction (at most 2 per cent, closed over half a second) waits while either deck is reversed, under a hand, braking or spinning up. A synced deck that takes over as master holds the tempo it was following (`heldTempoRate`) until its fader moves (`Command::tempoMoved`, which the session sends only while a tempo is held), and the BPM readout is the tempo. The session shows a held tempo past the fader's range on the narrowest range of 6, 10, 16 and 100 that reaches it, and the deck keeps that range when the fader is first touched, so the fader and the readout agree and the first touch moves nothing; synced across more than 6 per cent, which is common, the fader used to stand at the range's end and the first touch dropped the tempo to it. Choosing a range while a tempo is held hands the held tempo to the fader. The hold is a rate of the material it was held on, so a swap that does not keep the beat - a new track - drops it (`DjDeck::setSource`; `checkHeldTempoAcrossSwaps`, and `DjBoothEdges.inc` at the session).

## Slots and the hand-over

- **Stable slots.** Each deck plays in an engine slot that stays its own while it is on the console; `Session::DjBooth::slotOfDeck` holds the console's order, then the free slots. Taking a deck off empties its slot at the next block and moves nothing else: the decks after it close up on the console and play on untouched. Strips, the requested master and the beat effect's target name slots; a mixer channel past the console is the free slot a new deck would take, so the resonance and the fader curve the console sets on all six reach it first. A new deck's slot starts from a new deck's controls and strip, and a slot made active is emptied on the audio thread as one let go of is, so a deck starting there inherits nothing even when the slot's letting go never reached the queue. A beat effect on a deck taken off stays on the freed slot, which renders nothing, so it falls silent; the session used to write -1 there, which is the master, and the echo then ran on the whole mix.
- **Acknowledged, never predicted.** Material is owned on the message thread by the engine (`setDeckSource`) and crosses as a pointer in a command (`DjDeckSource`); a MIDI cell's notes ride inside it (`DjTrack::midi`). The deck lets material go (`letGo`) only when nothing on it reads it any more - its fade ended, superseded, its slot emptied - into a backlog the block hands over at its end to a ring of 64 (`DjReleaseQueue`); a full ring keeps the rest for the next block. `collect()` frees exactly what came back. The block counting it replaced freed a crossfade's source one block after the swap, a guess that a fade spanning blocks would have broken. `trackOf` is the material last handed to a slot, for the displays.
- **A full queue keeps what it refuses.** The queue drains only while blocks run, so it fills when the device has stopped. `setDeckSource` takes the material by rvalue reference and moves from it only when the command is queued, so refused it is still the caller's. The session keeps it on the deck (`DjBooth::Deck::waiting`, tagged with the load it came from) and the poll hands it over once there is room for it and for the settings queued behind it (`Session::handOverDjSource`); an eject waits the same way, and the poll lets go of any slot the engine still renders that no deck holds, which a `djReset` against a full queue leaves. Until the review of step 2 a refused load was destroyed and the deck marked stale, so it was bounced or read again after every quiet moment for as long as the queue stayed full.
- **The surface.** The message thread's settings are `DjDeckControls` (`reset`, `copyFrom`); commands are made by named factories (`Command::seek(slot, frame)`); the audio-thread methods are private to `DjEngine` and the test's `DjDeckTest`. The prepared rate, block and a generation each `prepare` bumps are atomics the session can read.

## The track port and the sequencer

Each slot has a `DjTrackPort` (`DjEngine::port`), the way a deck is heard through a track of the song (2026-10-12; [DJ decks play live through the track's chain](dj-decks-play-live-through-the-track.md)). It is two single-producer, single-consumer rings sized by `prepare` for four blocks, both ends on the audio device's thread: the **feed**, the deck's audio with its notes beside it as `DjPortEvent`s stamped with the feed frame they fall on (a ring of 1024), which the hidden DJ Input pops at the head of the track's chain; and the **tap**, the chain's output, which the hidden DJ Output pushes and the booth pops into the slot's strip. Neither side waits. A reader a block or two behind skips to the freshest block, dropping the note-ons it skipped and keeping their note-offs. A writer that finds no room for a block, or for its notes, drops them and raises an `overflowed` flag, since a note-off may be among them; the reader then empties the feed's audio and its notes, pops silence and reports `resynced`, the DJ Input lets go of every note it played, and the next block plays whole (until the review of 2026-10-12 the reader skipped on and delivered a stale block's note-ons whose note-offs were lost). So a graph or a booth that stopped running leaves no backlog and nothing sounding; `checkTrackPort` covers the skip and the resync. In steady state one block is in flight each way, so a track deck is a block late; an unbound slot goes through a `DjBlockDelay` instead, which gives back the previous block (silence when its size changed), so a file deck lines up with it. The port's `bound` atomic decides which path a slot takes; the session sets it after pointing the devices at the port, and a slot that changes sides starts its block delay afresh (`wentThroughPort`) rather than replay a stale block.

The sequencer (`DjEngine::sequence`) runs once a block per slot after the plan, before the port: the material's notes the deck passed in the block go out as events at their frames (at most 256 a block), a loop plays them round (its end played out before its start again), a note struck while it sounds is ended first, and every held note is let go when the deck stops, when it lands somewhere it was not travelling to, and when its material changes, since the new material may never end it. Notes before a landing are never replayed. An emptied slot clears its sequencer without sending note-offs: the track's DJ Input ends the notes it delivered itself, once the port is let go of. `checkTrackPort` in `tests/DjTest.cpp` plays the track's side as the devices do and holds each note to its block and frame. It is the knob-held preview's sequencer with a new destination: it fed `DjLiveSink`, the booth's queue to the message thread, while the bounce faded out under it (`previewGains`); the sink and the gains went with the preview.

## Denormals and cost

`DjSmoothedGain` snaps to its target within a millionth or once a step no longer moves it, so a fade lands on exactly 0 or 1 - heading for 1 with a 5 ms coefficient it used to stall some 7e-6 short - and the compressor's envelope is floored at 1e-9. `checkDenormals` reads the processor's own denormal flag (MXCSR) for both. The rest of the mixer still parks there in silence: a strip's isolator within ten blocks, the master's, the colour filter, and the send and beat effects later. The booth's callback runs under `ScopedNoDenormals`, so the app does not pay; the flushes are the mixer milestone's.

`checkBoothCost` times six decks, two playing synced in loops with every strip's compressor and filter, the send and the beat effect on, and counts allocations through the binary's own `operator new`, replaced in `tests/DjTest.cpp`: 0 in 2,000 blocks. Since the review of step 2 the counted window takes in the paths that hand material over and back, which steady playback never reaches: every hundred blocks a stopped deck takes new material, the master its material again by beat, the synced deck a quantised hot cue, the master a beat jump with its loop, and a slot is let go of and taken up again; the materials are made, handed over and collected outside the counter, as the message thread does all three, and the counter wraps each `process` alone. Per 512-frame block on this machine (2026-10-11), before step 2: 536 µs median, 795-801 µs at the 99th percentile, and 2,762-2,763 µs over 30 s of silence after a stop; after: 516-518 µs, 734-769 µs, 2,707 µs over 30 s, and 2,424-2,427 µs over the 10 s the committed check renders; the figures after the review, with the hand-overs in the window, were not recorded here. The check's decks are unbound, so it times the block delay and never a bound port. The silent tail is the mixer's denormals, nearly five times a playing block.
