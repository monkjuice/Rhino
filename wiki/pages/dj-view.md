# DJ view and the booth

The DJ view is the second view of the shell, opened by the Arrange/DJ switch in the control bar or by Tab (`MainComponent::setDjViewOpen`, `native/src/app/MainComponentPanes.cpp`). It took the place of the session view, a clip launcher that was paused and then removed on 2026-10-11 ([Clip slots, the DJ consoles' cells](clip-slots.md)). The user asked for it on 2026-10-09 as an experimental feature "oriented to DJs", with a CDJ-3000 and a DJM-V10 as the references.

## The engine is a second callback

The booth is `DjEngine`, pure C++ over JUCE core like the Drum Rack's engine, and `Session::DjBooth` wraps it in an `AudioIODeviceCallback` on the engine's own device manager, the arrangement the library preview and the count-in already use ([Browser and library preview](browser.md), [Recording and the count-in](recording.md)). JUCE sums its callbacks, so the booth is mixed with whatever the edit plays. That is what gives every deck a transport of its own: none of them is the song's, and the arrangement can stand still while six decks play.

- **Built on the first deck.** `Session::booth()` makes the booth and `ensureDjAttached` puts it on the device the first time a deck is added, so a session that never opens the DJ view registers no callback; `releaseAudioDevice` and the destructor take it off (`releaseDj`) before the device goes. Under the test runners (`isCommandLineTestMode`) it never goes on the device (since 2026-10-11): they drive it themselves through `djProcessOffline`, and a device thread calling the same callback meanwhile was a second consumer of the engine's single-consumer queue and a second renderer of its decks. The callback runs under `juce::ScopedNoDenormals`, which covers the offline drive too.
- **Commands cross in a single-producer queue** (`DjEngine::Command`, 256 deep) and are applied at the start of a block; the deck's position is changed on the audio thread and nowhere else. What a deck is doing is read back from atomics (`Session::djDeckState`). Knob and fader moves are atomics the audio thread reads once a block and smooths ([Real-time audio rules](real-time-audio-rules.md)).
- **Material is handed over by pointer, in a command** (`DjEngine::setDeckSource`), applied with every other command at the top of the block, converting the deck's position, cues and loop by beat when asked (`keepBeat`), which is what a cell made again wants. The engine owns it on the message thread until the audio thread hands it back through a ring, which it does only once nothing reads it - its crossfade ended, superseded, its slot emptied - and `collect()` frees exactly what came back. `DjEngine::prepare`, which a device restart runs, lets nothing go. The queue drains only while blocks run, so with the device stopped it fills: a load arriving then keeps its material on the deck, still loading, and the poll hands it over once there is room. See [DJ deck engine](dj-deck-engine.md).
- **Each deck has an engine slot of its own** while it is on the console; the session keeps the console's order (`DjBooth::slotOfDeck`) and every public `Session::dj*` call names a deck by its place there.
- **Positions are frames of the track at its own rate**, so a hot cue survives a device at another rate. The player reads with four-point Hermite interpolation; the tempo fader, the jog's push and sync all change the read rate (vinyl-style: pitch moves with tempo), smoothed over 30 ms. Key lock (pitch-held stretching) is not built.
- **The audio callback's scratch buffer holds this callback's previous block** (see `CountInClick`), so `DjEngine::process` clears before it writes. A block bigger than `prepare` was told is worked in pieces.
- The tests need no audio device: `DjTest.cpp` drives `DjEngine::process` directly, and the scenarios drive the booth through `Session::djProcessOffline`, which calls the booth's own `audioDeviceIOCallbackWithContext` with whatever inputs the test hands it (the overload taking inputs; the one-argument form passes none) and then collects, so what the device would hand the booth is what the test hands it.

## What a deck does

`DjDeck` is a CDJ's transport: play/pause with 2 ms fades; Cue as a CDJ reads it (playing: stop and return; stopped at the cue point: play while held; stopped elsewhere: set it here); eight hot cues (press empty to set, press set to jump and play, right-click to clear); loop in/out, beat loops of 1/8 to 32 beats, reloop/exit, halve and double; beat jump, exactly so many beats from where the deck stands, which carries an active loop with it when the loop fits; seek; reverse; and the end of the track stops it through its fade. A loop may end at the material's length and wraps for ever; any landing outside an active loop leaves it; and a jump, a wrap, a change of material and an eject crossfade over 5 ms from what the deck was playing ([DJ deck engine](dj-deck-engine.md)). Quantize is per deck (`quantiseSnap`, the console's Q key): cue points and loop points snap to the track's nearest beat while it is on. Cue search (`Session::djJumpToCue`) moves to the previous or next cue point, hot cues and the cue alike, and back past the first is the top of the track.

**The brake** (`DjDeck::brakeSeconds`, 0-4 s, the CDJ's vinyl speed adjust) winds a stop down from the rate the deck was at to nothing over that time, and spins a start up from nothing the same way, so a half-second stop covers a quarter of a second of material; at zero both are at once. **The hand on the platter** (`scratching`, `scratchRate`) sets the rate outright, backwards included, and a hand that rests holds the deck still, until it lets go and the fader's rate comes back; the engine reads it in `setRates`, ahead of the tempo fader and sync. Both are measured in `tests/DjTest.cpp`.

**Quantised starts** are the Live mechanism the user asked for, and optional. `DjEngine::Quantise` is off, beat, bar or four bars (a bar is the master's own `beatsPerBar`). A start or a hot cue jump is held (`DjDeck::Pending`, shown as a blinking Play key) and keeps its target exactly: it lands at the frame where the master's phase within the boundary reaches the target's phase on the deck's own grid, so bars line up with bars and no cue is moved; the master's own hot cues land against its own grid, so its phase runs on through them. The master is read on through its loop as it will play it, and a loop that never reaches the target's place in the bar lands it on the beat's phase, or at the loop's wrap; a synced deck counts the master's beats folded as its tempo is. With nothing else playing, or quantise off, it lands at once. Until step 2 of the live-chain work (2026-10-11) the target was snapped to the deck's nearest bar, a jump landed at the block's start, and the master's own jumps at once; until its review, a master looping a beat off the bar line never landed a bar-line cue.

**Master and sync.** The master is the deck chosen with its Master key while it plays, else the first playing deck in slot order; when it stops the next playing deck takes over (`chooseMaster`). A synced deck plays at the master's tempo - its fader and push, never its platter, so a braked, scratched or reversed master drags nobody - folded by an octave to the nearest (87 BPM against 174 plays at its own tempo), and with phase lock on (default) is nudged back onto the master's beat whenever it drifts by more than a millisecond, by at most two per cent of rate, while both decks run steadily. A synced deck that takes over as master keeps the tempo it was playing at until its fader moves, the fader showing it on a range widened to reach it, so the first touch moves nothing; a new track on it plays at its own fader's tempo. Pressing Sync also jumps the deck onto the master's phase within the bar, without leaving a loop it plays in.

## The mixer

`DjChannelStrip` follows the DJM-V10's channel, which the user asked for on 2026-10-09 with the V10's manual as the reference: trim (±12 dB); the one-knob **compressor** (`DjCompressor`: the knob brings the threshold down from 0 to −30 dBFS and the ratio up to 4:1, with six tenths of the loss made up, so a thin track fills out — at full, a −6 dBFS tone is held down about 4.5 dB and a −40 dBFS one lifted about 13 dB); the four-band **EQ** (`DjIsolator` with three Linkwitz-Riley fourth-order crossovers at 150 Hz, 600 Hz and 3 kHz; each band from a full kill, `DjIsolator::killDb`, to +6 dB — the two inner bands are only two octaves wide, so a kill in the middle of one leaves what leaks in from its neighbours, around −19 dB, and the tests ask the outer bands for −30 dB and the inner ones for −15); the one-knob **colour filter** (a state-variable filter: low-pass closing from 20 kHz to 60 Hz left of centre, high-pass opening from 20 Hz to 10 kHz right of it, dead in the middle); the **send** to the send/return unit, taken after the fader; a meter after the EQ and before the fader; the fader with a **curve** (`faderGainFor`: gentle is the square root of the position, normal the square, steep the fourth power; one key in the master section sets every channel's); cue; the effect assign; and a crossfader side (A, through, B). The crossfader's curve is three-way: a smooth equal-power blend, a straighter one, and a sharp cut for scratching.

The master section (`DjMasterSection`) is the level with a soft clip over the last decibel, a three-band isolator at 250 Hz and 3 kHz, and the headphones: the cue bus against the master (`cueMix`), their own level, and mono split (the cue in the left ear, the master in the right), on outputs 3 and 4 when the device has them. The **mic** (`DjMicSection`) is the audio device's first input channel, handed to `DjEngine::process` by the booth's callback (`DjBooth::audioDeviceIOCallbackWithContext` in `SessionDj.cpp`): ±12 dB shelves and three modes, off, on, and talkover, which ducks the master by 18 dB while the mic is spoken into and lets it back up as the mic falls quiet. The engine's three-argument `process` passes no inputs, and the callback called that form until the wiki keeper's check of 2026-10-09 caught it: the engine's own test drove the overload and could not tell. `Session::djProcessOffline` now drives the booth through the very same callback, and `DjBooth.inc` feeds a tone through it and reads the mic's meter. The **send/return unit** (`DjSendFx`) takes the channels' sends: a short delay (5-120 ms), a long delay, a dub echo (whose repeats lose their bottom as well as their top) or a reverb, with size (feedback, or decay), time (a delay's time glides, a thousandth of the way each sample, so a knob turn bends rather than clicks; a test measuring the return runs about 100 blocks after setting the type, or the click comes back early), tone and the level the return comes back into the master at.

`DjBeatFx` is one unit on one channel or the master, timed in beats of the master tempo, or of the tapped tempo (`DjEngine::tapBpm`, 128 until the TAP key is pressed) when nothing plays, or by its own TIME knob with Auto off. Fourteen kinds: delay and echo (both ring out after they are switched off), ping-pong, spiral and helix (feedback delays whose repeats climb two and seven semitones through `DjPitchShifter`), reverb and shimmer, flanger, phaser, a swept filter, trans (a gate), roll (captures the beats after it is switched on and repeats them), pitch (−12 to +12 whole semitones over the depth knob) and vinyl brake (the sound winds down to nothing over the beat). The band keys (`bandLow`, `bandMid`, `bandHigh`, the DJM's FX FREQUENCY) keep the dry signal in the bands switched off: the effect's change is split by a three-band isolator and only the lit bands' share is added back, so a tone in a dark band passes untouched. The swept effects take their phase from the master's beat count, so they lock to the beat.

## What a deck plays

- **A file** is read whole and analysed on the booth's one worker thread, up to fifteen minutes, and `Session::djPoll` installs it. Any format JUCE's basic formats read. It plays through no track, delayed one block so that it lines up with the track decks beside it.
- **A track of the song** (`loadDjDeckTrack`) makes the deck that track's: from then on it is heard through the track's chain (below), and it plays nothing until one of the track's cells is chosen (`DjDeckInfo::slot` −1). The arrangement's clips are never a deck's. A group bus or a return track is refused (`carriesOtherTracks`), and so is a track another deck holds: one chain plays one deck. A cell (`loadDjDeckSlot`) is made into material by `loadDjClip`: a MIDI clip at once, on the message thread, as its notes over its length rounded up to whole bars on the song's grid, with no audio of its own (`DjTrack::silentFrames`: the track's instrument makes the sound); an audio clip as its stretch of file, read on the worker, padded with silence to whole bars and analysed with the song's tempo as the known grid. A fresh cell loops whole. Choosing the cell a deck already plays, or is reading, does nothing (until the review of 2026-10-12 it dropped the read in flight as stale and then skipped the remake, stranding the deck). A deck already on the track keeps its beat across a switch, and one moving to another track keeps playing until the new cell lands: at once for a MIDI cell, while for an audio cell its old material is heard through the new track's chain until the worker's read lands (emptying the deck first stopped a playing deck, and was reverted).
- **Live** switches a track deck's monitoring On ([Track inputs and monitoring](inputs-and-monitoring.md)), so its instrument plays from the keys, heard through the deck's channel like everything the chain makes; a Drum Rack on it plays its pads. The monitoring is put back when Live goes off. The key is enabled on any track deck; the model refuses a file deck, an audio track and a MIDI track running no instrument. A refusal from arming (no audio device, an input the machine lacks) leaves the deck live all the same: the track took the setting and will be heard once there is an input.
- **Edit** opens the cell the deck plays (`Session::djDeckEditClip`), in the note editor for a MIDI clip and the audio editor for an audio one, and nothing while no cell plays; never an arrangement clip. The control bar's Clip toggle opens the same clip ([App shell and control bar](app-shell.md)).
- **Removing a deck closes the gap** (`removeDjDeck`): the deck's engine slot is emptied, its track plays the song again, and nothing else moves; the decks after it close up on the console and in the mixer and play on from where they stood, strips, settings and the beat effect's assign with them, none read or made again. A beat effect on the deck taken off stays on its emptied slot, a channel past the console, and falls silent; a deck added there later starts with its FX key dark. Until the review of step 2 (2026-10-11) the effect moved onto the master and echoed the whole mix.

## Heard through the track

`bindDjTrack(slot, track)` points the track's hidden ends at the slot's `DjTrackPort` - the ends first, then the port's `bound` flag, so the audio thread never finds a bound port with nowhere to go - and, outside the test runners, calls `ensureContextAllocated`, since the track's graph has to run for the deck to be heard. Every block the booth plans and reads the deck, turns the notes the deck passed into events at their frames, pushes audio and events into the port and takes back what the chain gave a block before ([DJ deck engine](dj-deck-engine.md)). On the song's side the **DJ Input** (plugin 0) adds the deck's audio to the block and its notes to the chain's MIDI, so MIDI effects and the instrument play them, an audio track's effects work the deck's sound, and a monitored input still plays; it answers its input count of channels, two at a chain's head with none, so a mono chain stays mono. The **DJ Output**, where the track's own devices end and its channel strip begins (Utility, Volume & Pan and a level meter, in an order that depends on how the track was made: [Device chain order](device-chain-order.md)), pushes the chain's output into the port and clears the block, so the song's output carries none of a track a deck holds and the song's trim, fader, pan and mute stay the song's. Both pass the block through unless bound, and whenever the song's transport rolls or a render runs (`context.isPlaying`, `isRendering`): the song owns its tracks then, and a merge or an export never hears a deck. Why they are permanent devices, and what that costs: [DJ decks play live through the track's chain](dj-decks-play-live-through-the-track.md).

- **No note is left sounding.** The input remembers the notes it delivered and ends them in the chain's MIDI the moment it stops playing the deck: unbound (an eject, a removal, a move to another track), the song's transport rolling, a render, or the port resyncing after an overflow ([DJ deck engine](dj-deck-engine.md)). Until the review of 2026-10-12 a deck ejected, removed, moved to another track or overridden by the song left its notes sounding on the old track's instrument.
- **The song's mute and solo leave a deck its track.** `RhinoEngineBehaviour::shouldProcessMutedTracks()` returns true (`SessionInternal.h`), so a muted or solo-excluded track still runs its chain, hidden ends included, and only its output to the song is silenced; the engine used to skip the whole chain. The price is the CPU a muted track's devices take ([Mixer](mixer.md)).
- **A device asks for nothing.** `djDocumentChanged` marks a deck's material dirty only for `notes` or `audioClips` on its track, or for `tempo`, which moves every cell's grid, and only on a deck playing a cell; parameters, curves and devices are heard live. `djPoll` makes dirty material again only while `gestureHeld()` is false (a knob, fader, tempo, audio clip or note-velocity gesture, held V included, or any mouse button down), so a note being dragged is not cut on every step. A deck keeps a `clipSignature` - the clip, its notes or its stretch of file, its length, the tempo and the bar's length in beats, so a time-signature change remakes the cell - and a remake that comes out the same hands nothing over: an edit to another cell of the track, or to the arrangement, leaves the deck alone (`ClipCells.inc`). A remake still on the worker when other material lands is re-stamped with the new generation (`handOverDjSource`) rather than dropped as stale.
- **No end is left reading a port.** `bindDjTrack` points away every hidden end, on any track, that points at the slot's port but the new track's, so a track an undo brought back with its old devices never keeps a second reader on a port; `djUnbindAll` (`~Session`, `djReset`) clears every hidden end of every track. The poll asserts each binding again by the track's `EditItemID`. A deck whose track is deleted lets go of it and of its material, which would otherwise play on raw through the booth, and shows "The deck's track is no longer in the song."; an undo that brings the track back has the poll bind it again and make its cell again.
- **Limits.** A track deck is a block late, and file decks are delayed to match. An audio cell plays its file's raw stretch, without the clip's gain, pan, pitch, fades, reverse or warp. A deck moving to another track's audio cell plays its old material through the new track's chain until the read lands. While a deck holds a track and the song is stopped, the track is no sidechain carrier ([Rhino Vocoder and sidechains](rhino-vocoder.md)) and an audio track's monitored input is heard through the deck's channel, not the song's.
- **Proven through Tracktion's real graph** (2026-10-12). It all rests on the song's graph running a stopped track's plugins once a playback context exists: play-in-stop (`Edit::playInStopEnabled`, true by default; `TransportControl::performStop` keeps the nodes). `DjLiveChain.inc`'s scenario "dj live: the song's own graph plays a deck's track while the song stands still" drives the engine's own graph through its hosted audio device beside the booth and hears it: the deck plays the instrument with the song stopped and the song's output silent, nothing while the song rolls, the track back once it stops, a muted track and a solo-excluded one still on the deck, and nothing left sounding into the song after an eject ([Tracktion's hosted device is silent until its start is dispatched](hosted-device-silent-until-dispatched.md)). The scenarios that play the chain by hand remain, for levels and knobs.

**History.** Until 2026-10-12 a track deck played a bounce of the arrangement (the SONG cell) or of a cell, rendered on the booth's worker from a copy of the document and rendered again half a second after an edit it could hear ([superseded](dj-bounce-on-a-copy.md)); a group could be bounced onto a deck; Reload bounced at once, and its right-click switched the automatic re-bounce off; and while a knob was held the bounce faded out and its notes were injected into the track from the message thread, which silenced the deck's channel strip. All of it went for the live chain after the user's report of 2026-10-11.

## The analysis

`analyseDjTrack` makes the picture and the numbers from a float buffer. One pass fills a waveform column per 256 frames in three bands (lows under 180 Hz, highs over 2.5 kHz) and, per column, the energy of four bands. The tempo comes from an onset envelope (log-compressed rises of the band energies, local mean removed) by autocorrelation over 60-200 BPM, scored with its octaves and a mild preference around 128, then a comb search that settles the period to five thousandths of a BPM and finds the phase. The band energies for that envelope are averaged over 46 ms: a column is shorter than one cycle of a kick, so a column's own energy rose and fell with the sine's phase and the ripple correlated with itself every 0.4 beats and buried the beat (the analyser read 158.2 BPM for a synthesised 126.5 BPM beat; a Python replica of the arithmetic, printing the correlation curve, is what showed the ripple, see [Measure sound, don't read the DSP](measure-sound-dont-read-dsp.md)). The phase is then read from the unsmoothed flux, which spikes in the column each attack lands in. The downbeat is the beat of the four the low band hits hardest. Bars are measured as RMS from the first downbeat, and a drop is a bar that is loud, half again as loud as the four before it and plainly louder than the one before, eight bars apart at least. The key is a chroma profile (a 4096-point transform every 8192 frames, 55 Hz to 2.2 kHz, lower partials weighted) against the Krumhansl-Schmuckler profiles, named as a DJ reads it with its Camelot number. A cell's grid is the song's: a MIDI cell's is set outright, and an audio cell is analysed with the song's tempo as `knownBpm`.

## The view

`DjView` stands the decks either side of the mixer, odd to the left and even to the right, up to three a column. **It syncs the mixer before it lays the decks out**, because the layout reads the mixer's width from its strips: laid out first, a fourth deck's strip sat under the master section until the window was next resized (reported 2026-10-09; [A layout that reads a child's preferred size must sync the child first](sync-children-before-layout.md)).

`DjDeckPanel` is laid out as a CDJ-3000 is. The name bar chooses the source (the song's tracks, a file chooser, the library's samples by pack, the machine's drives) or takes a dropped file, and on a deck playing a track of the song carries Live and Edit, Edit only while a cell plays. Under it the screen, then the eight hot cues. The body is three columns. To the left: the loop keys (in, out, reloop/exit, the beat loop with its length on a right-click, halve and double), the beat jump with its size, cue search, Reverse and the deck's quantize key, with the big round Cue and Play beneath. In the middle the **jog wheel** (`DjJogWheel` in `DjControls.h`): a platter whose marker turns with the track at 33⅓ rpm, a ring of ticks, the track's name in the window, and a vinyl-mode key and the brake knob under it. In vinyl mode a hand on the platter scratches — the hand's angular speed, in turns of 1.8 s, becomes the deck's rate, and a hand that rests holds the record still, a 30 Hz timer noticing the rest — and a standing deck is scrubbed; the ring, or the platter in CDJ mode, nudges the tempo. To the right Sync, Master, the tempo range, the tempo fader (the CDJ's way, down is faster, with a centre detent and a scale) and Reset. The screen takes about a third of the console (32 %) and no more than 220 px since 2026-10-10 (it took whatever the keys left, which on a tall window was most of the console), and gives that share back down to its 76 px minimum - the readouts, the overview strip and a sliver of waveform - before the keys lose their full size; the body - the keys, the jog wheel and the clips grid - takes the rest, and at 1240×720 the clips grid shows all eight cells and the + row. A short console squeezes its key rows before it touches that minimum, and a short or narrow one drops the jog wheel first, giving it and its two controls empty bounds so the test that wants every key inside the console still holds. Every key with a second action on a right-click (a hot cue, Eject, Beat Loop) is a `DjPad`, never a stock button ([A juce::Button fires its click on a right-click too](juce-button-fires-on-right-click.md)). A press anywhere on the console focuses its deck: the panel is its own deep mouse listener and drops the second hearing of a direct press by event time ([A JUCE component listening to itself hears its own clicks twice](juce-self-listener-hears-clicks-twice.md)). The focused deck wears the selection strip and a lit number in its name bar; `DjView::focusDeck` marks it and tells the shell (`deckSelected` carries the deck's index), the shell's lower pane follows its track ([App shell and control bar](app-shell.md)), and `togglePlayFocused` is what Space does in this view, where the song's transport is dimmed.

`DjMixerPanel` occupies the full height of the centre column. Its upper mixer is clamped between 520 and 620 px when the view has room, with the crossfader at that upper section's foot; the SEND FX and BEAT FX controls form a labelled rack beneath it and take the remaining height. This uses the centre space that was blank before 2026-10-16 without making a short window unusable. The rack split also leaves the master section as two wider columns: master level and isolator with the stereo meters, filter resonance and channel-fader curve; then headphones (mix, level, mono split) and mic (level, two shelves, off/on/talkover, a meter). The rack holds the send unit (type, size, time, tone, mix) and the beat effect (type, target, three band keys, the beat between ◀ and ▶, AUTO and TAP, TIME and DEPTH, ON/OFF). Its width follows the strips, which narrow when six of them and the master section would not fit half the view. The useful-size targets are 80 px per strip, 56 px minimum, 32 px knobs, 20 px keys and 16 px titles; the workflow scenario pins the full-height centre, FX below the crossfader and these larger controls. A strip is, top to bottom, what the V10's channel is: TRIM, COMP, HI, HI MID, LOW MID, LOW, FILTER, SEND, the CUE and FX keys, the meter beside the fader, and the A/THRU/B key. The tempo readout at the top of the master column shows the master deck's BPM, or the tapped tempo when nothing plays, repainted only when its text changes. `DjDeckDisplay` scrolls the waveform under a fixed playhead from tiles painted once at the current zoom (the wheel zooms, 3-48 s across), draws the grid, the loop and the cue flags on top each frame, shows the whole track in a strip below with its drops marked and the played part dimmed (a click seeks), and a line of readouts repainted only when their text changes. A MIDI cell's material shows its notes instead (`DjDeckDisplay::paintTile`, from `DjTrack::midi`, since 2026-10-10): a row per pitch over the pitches the material uses, brighter for a harder velocity, a note ending at the next note-off of its pitch or a quarter beat on; it carries no audio, so its overview strip is flat. While a load is in flight the deck plays on what it had, and the screen and the keys go on showing it (`DjDeckDisplay::sync` takes the engine's track while `loading`; `DjDeckPanel::sync` counts a loading deck with material as loaded). Dragging the scrolling strip pushes the tempo while the button is down, as a hand on the platter does. The view's displays move on the display's refresh, its keys and meters at 30 Hz, and the shell's timer polls the loads. Hidden, the view marks itself stale as the other faces do ([What a change costs the interface](ui-cost-of-a-change.md)). Meaning colours are `palette::dj*` in `Theme.h`. A deck's remove key is answered through `MessageManager::callAsync` (`DjView::removeDeck`), because the request comes from a key on the panel the removal destroys.

## The clips grid

Each console carries its track's clip slots as a column of cells (`DjClipGrid`; the model under it is [Clip slots, the DJ consoles' cells](clip-slots.md)), scene 0 at the top: the SONG cell that stood above them, the track's arrangement, went on 2026-10-12. The column stands beside the big Cue and Play keys when the console's left column is at least the key size plus 70 px wide, else above them, and takes empty bounds when not even one cell fits, for the test that reads every child ([Writing Rhino tests](writing-rhino-tests.md)). A deck plays one cell at a time (`Session::loadDjDeckSlot`, `DjDeckInfo::slot`, saved as the deck's `slot`), and the lit cell is the one it plays. A press on a filled cell loads it; a double-click on an empty cell makes a one-bar MIDI clip there (`createSlotClip`, named for its cell; an audio track's cell refuses with a hint to drop a sample), loads it and opens it in the note editor, so the notes go in while the deck turns and each change plays after the next poll; a double-click on a filled cell opens it; the row past the last scene makes a scene. A cell's clip wears its track's colour, darkened as on the timeline, unless it carries a colour of its own, as a copy of a coloured timeline clip does (`SlotClip::colour` is `Session::clipColour` since 2026-10-11; it was the engine's made-up colour). A right-click deletes a clip, and a deck that played it is left its track's, playing nothing; the delete repairs the note editor's clip (`repairPatternClip`, as the timeline's delete does, and as a copy over a filled cell and a sample or a pattern dropped on one now do), because a console opens a slot clip there and the editor painted a freed clip otherwise - the crash of 2026-10-10 ([Hold ids, not pointers](ids-not-pointers.md)). A copy into a cell takes a name no other clip on the track wears, slots and timeline alike (`uniqueClipNameOnTrack`: a copy of "Clip 1" beside it is "Clip 2", of "Break" is "Break 2"; `copyClipInto` sets the name again after the MIDI clone, which carries the source's name across), and a new cell's "Clip N" is made unique the same way. A cell on a MIDI track with no instrument plays at once and sounds nothing until an instrument arrives, which is heard at once with nothing made again; the bounce needed a special case for it (silence of the span, 2026-10-10), the live chain none.

Clips are separate from the arrangement's, and every route across copies (`ClipDrag.h` carries the descriptions). A cell dragged leaves as `rhino-slot:<track>:<scene>` with a small tile for a drag image, and lands on another console's cell (`copyClipToTrackSlot`, held to the lane's kind) or, through the Arrange switch, on a lane of the timeline (`copySlotClipToArrangement` with a target lane). A timeline clip carried up out of the arrangement, past the band where the lanes scroll, leaves as `rhino-clip:<id>` - the arrangement cancels its own move first, so the clip stays put - and lands on a cell as a copy. The control bar's two view toggles are drop targets (`ViewToggle`) that show their view while a clip or a browser item hovers them, which is how a drag crosses. Library items land on a cell by the track's kind, as on a lane: a sound file, a library sample among them, makes an audio clip on an audio track and is refused on a MIDI track, except that a track running a Drum Rack takes it onto a pad (`rhino-browser:file:` only; a built-in `sample:` goes to `insertBuiltInSampleInSlot` and is refused on any MIDI track); a pattern makes a MIDI clip; and an instrument, effect, preset or kit joins the track's chain without making a clip. A drop on the + row, or on a full grid, makes a scene only when it makes a clip: the model grows the scenes inside the insert, after its refusals, so a device or a refused sample adds no row (2026-10-11; the grid used to add the scene first). A drop on the console beside the grid goes into the first free cell and plays. A deck playing a file has no track of the song: its grid is hidden and a dropped file is its material.

`Session::findClip` finds a clip in a slot as well as on the timeline since 2026-10-10, which is what lets the note editor and the audio editor open a slot clip, and `trackHoldingClip` (`SessionInternal.h`) answers for the track of a clip whose own lookup knows only the timeline. [Clip slots, the DJ consoles' cells](clip-slots.md) records the consequence.

## What the document saves

`rhinoDj` on the edit's state, written when a snapshot is taken (`projectSnapshot`) and read back on restore: each deck's source (a file by stored path, a track by its `EditItemID` with the `slot` it plays, −1 for none), tempo settings, brake and quantize, cue, hot cues and loop in seconds, Live and `liveRestore` (the monitoring Live replaced: the track itself is saved monitoring On, and Live switched off after a reopen puts the saved mode back, where until 2026-10-12 it left the track On); every channel strip, compressor, four bands, send and fader curve included; the crossfader and its curve; the master, the headphones' level and mono split; the mic; the send unit; the effect with its bands, time mode and manual time; the tapped tempo; quantise and phase lock. Positions are not saved. A reopened deck is empty; the poll binds a track deck to its track again and makes its cell again, or reads its file, and its cues are applied once the material is there (`applyDjDeckSettings`); a live deck saved without `liveRestore` switches Live on when the material lands. Adding, removing or ejecting a deck, loading a file, a track or a cell, setting or clearing a hot cue and changing quantise mark the document modified; knob and fader moves do not, and nothing here is an undo step. Those marks go through `markDjModified`, which is `markModified(-1, ModelChange::none)`: it bumps the revision and the edit's changed flag and asks nothing of any deck.

## Decisions

- **A track's deck plays through the track's live chain** (2026-10-12), not a render of it: [DJ decks play live through the track's chain](dj-decks-play-live-through-the-track.md). It replaced "decks play audio, not the edit" and the bounce of [the superseded decision](dj-bounce-on-a-copy.md). A deck still keeps a transport of its own: the booth plays the material and sequences the notes, and only the track's devices are borrowed.
- **One engine, one callback,** rather than a Tracktion edit per deck: a second edit per deck would have cost a graph each and still needed the mixer written.
- **Vinyl-style tempo** (pitch moves with speed) for now; a pitch-held mode needs the engine's stretcher on the booth's thread and is noted as not built.
- **Rhino's own mixer DSP** rather than the edit's plugins, so the mixer is measured offline and never rebuilds a playback graph.

## Not built

Key lock; slip mode; a sampler; recording the master; MIDI control of the booth; a chooser for the mic's input (the booth takes the device's first input channel); the booth's own output routing (booth out, the V10's second output); an audio cell's own clip settings on a deck (gain, pan, pitch, fades, reverse, warp: the deck reads the file's raw stretch). Making the booth's mixer itself a hidden effect device in the chain was weighed and would buy nothing: the strips are cheap and already run on the audio thread, in the booth's own callback.
