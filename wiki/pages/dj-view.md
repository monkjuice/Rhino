# DJ view and the booth

The DJ view is the second view of the shell, opened by the Arrange/DJ switch in the control bar or by Tab (`sessionViewEnabled` in `native/src/Main.cpp`, now true). It took the place the paused clip launcher had ([Session view (paused)](session-view.md)); that view is still built and tested but nothing in the shell reaches it. The user asked for it on 2026-10-09 as an experimental feature "oriented to DJs", with a CDJ-3000 and a DJM-V10 as the references.

| Layer | Files |
| --- | --- |
| DSP (`RhinoCore`, JUCE core only) | `core/DjEngine.*` (the booth: decks into a mixer, the command queue, quantised starts, sync), `core/DjDeck.*` (one player), `core/DjMixer.*` (channel strips, isolator, colour filter, crossfader, master, beat effect), `core/DjAnalysis.*` (waveform columns, tempo and grid, bars and drops, key), `core/DjTrack.h` (a deck's material) |
| Model | `SessionDj.cpp` (decks, transport, mixer, what the document saves), `SessionDjSources.cpp` (files read on a worker, tracks and groups bounced, Live, the stale re-bounce), `SessionDjInternal.h` (the booth and its audio callback) |
| UI | `DjView.*` (the view), `DjDeckPanel.*` (a deck's console), `DjDeckDisplay.*` (its screen), `DjClipGrid.*` (its clips grid), `DjMixerPanel.*` (the mixer), `DjControls.h` (pads, faders, meters), `ClipDrag.h` (the drag descriptions a clip crosses the views by, shared with the arrangement) |
| Tests | `tests/DjTest.cpp` (`--self-test`), `tests/Arrangement/scenarios/DjBooth.inc` |

## The engine is a second callback

The booth is `DjEngine`, pure C++ over JUCE core like the Drum Rack's engine, and `Session::DjBooth` wraps it in an `AudioIODeviceCallback` on the engine's own device manager, the arrangement the library preview and the count-in already use ([Browser and library preview](browser.md), [Recording and the count-in](recording.md)). JUCE sums its callbacks, so the booth is mixed with whatever the edit plays. That is what gives every deck a transport of its own: none of them is the song's, and the arrangement can stand still while six decks play.

- **Built on the first deck.** `Session::booth()` makes the booth and `ensureDjAttached` puts it on the device the first time a deck is added, so a session that never opens the DJ view registers no callback; `releaseAudioDevice` and the destructor take it off (`releaseDj`) before the device goes.
- **Commands cross in a single-producer queue** (`DjEngine::Command`, 256 deep) and are applied at the start of a block; the deck's position is changed on the audio thread and nowhere else. What a deck is doing is read back from atomics (`Session::djDeckState`). Knob and fader moves are atomics the audio thread reads once a block and smooths ([Real-time audio rules](real-time-audio-rules.md)).
- **Material is handed over by pointer** (`DjEngine::setTrack`) and freed by `collect()` once as many blocks have ended as had begun when it was let go of, the way the Drum Rack counts its samples out. A deck adopts a swapped track at its next block or its next command, converting its position, cues and loop by beat when asked (`keepBeatOnSwap`), which is what a re-bounce wants.
- **Positions are frames of the track at its own rate**, so a hot cue survives a device at another rate. The player reads with four-point Hermite interpolation; the tempo fader, the jog's push and sync all change the read rate (vinyl-style: pitch moves with tempo), smoothed over 30 ms. Key lock (pitch-held stretching) is not built.
- **The audio callback's scratch buffer holds this callback's previous block** (see `CountInClick`), so `DjEngine::process` clears before it writes. A block bigger than `prepare` was told is worked in pieces.
- The tests need no audio device: `DjTest.cpp` drives `DjEngine::process` directly, and the scenarios drive the booth through `Session::djProcessOffline`, which calls the booth's own `audioDeviceIOCallbackWithContext` with whatever inputs the test hands it (the overload taking inputs; the one-argument form passes none) and then collects, so what the device would hand the booth is what the test hands it.

## What a deck does

`DjDeck` is a CDJ's transport: play/pause with 2 ms fades; Cue as a CDJ reads it (playing: stop and return; stopped at the cue point: play while held; stopped elsewhere: set it here); eight hot cues (press empty to set, press set to jump and play, right-click to clear); loop in/out, beat loops of 1/8 to 32 beats, reloop/exit, halve and double; beat jump, which carries an active loop with it; seek; reverse; and the end of the track stops it. Quantize is per deck (`quantiseSnap`, the console's Q key): cue points and loop points snap to the track's nearest beat while it is on. Cue search (`Session::djJumpToCue`) moves to the previous or next cue point, hot cues and the cue alike, and back past the first is the top of the track.

**The brake** (`DjDeck::brakeSeconds`, 0-4 s, the CDJ's vinyl speed adjust) winds a stop down from the rate the deck was at to nothing over that time, and spins a start up from nothing the same way, so a half-second stop covers a quarter of a second of material; at zero both are at once. **The hand on the platter** (`scratching`, `scratchRate`) sets the rate outright, backwards included, and a hand that rests holds the deck still, until it lets go and the fader's rate comes back; the engine reads it in `setRates`, ahead of the tempo fader and sync. Both are measured in `tests/DjTest.cpp`.

**Quantised starts** are the Live mechanism the user asked for, and optional. `DjEngine::Quantise` is off, beat, bar or four bars. A start or a hot cue jump on a deck other than the master is held (`DjDeck::Pending`, shown as a blinking Play key) until the master's next boundary falls inside a block, then lands at that frame, snapped to the deck's own boundary of the same size (a bar at most), so bars line up with bars. With nothing else playing, or quantise off, it lands at once.

**Master and sync.** The master is the deck chosen with its Master key while it plays, else the first playing deck; when it stops the next playing deck takes over (`chooseMaster`). A synced deck plays at the master's effective tempo and, with phase lock on (default), is nudged back onto the master's beat whenever it drifts by more than a millisecond, by at most two per cent of rate. Pressing Sync also jumps the deck onto the master's phase within the bar.

## The mixer

`DjChannelStrip` follows the DJM-V10's channel, which the user asked for on 2026-10-09 with the V10's manual as the reference: trim (±12 dB); the one-knob **compressor** (`DjCompressor`: the knob brings the threshold down from 0 to −30 dBFS and the ratio up to 4:1, with six tenths of the loss made up, so a thin track fills out — at full, a −6 dBFS tone is held down about 4.5 dB and a −40 dBFS one lifted about 13 dB); the four-band **EQ** (`DjIsolator` with three Linkwitz-Riley fourth-order crossovers at 150 Hz, 600 Hz and 3 kHz; each band from a full kill, `DjIsolator::killDb`, to +6 dB — the two inner bands are only two octaves wide, so a kill in the middle of one leaves what leaks in from its neighbours, around −19 dB, and the tests ask the outer bands for −30 dB and the inner ones for −15); the one-knob **colour filter** (a state-variable filter: low-pass closing from 20 kHz to 60 Hz left of centre, high-pass opening from 20 Hz to 10 kHz right of it, dead in the middle); the **send** to the send/return unit, taken after the fader; a meter after the EQ and before the fader; the fader with a **curve** (`faderGainFor`: gentle is the square root of the position, normal the square, steep the fourth power; one key in the master section sets every channel's); cue; the effect assign; and a crossfader side (A, through, B). The crossfader's curve is three-way: a smooth equal-power blend, a straighter one, and a sharp cut for scratching.

The master section (`DjMasterSection`) is the level with a soft clip over the last decibel, a three-band isolator at 250 Hz and 3 kHz, and the headphones: the cue bus against the master (`cueMix`), their own level, and mono split (the cue in the left ear, the master in the right), on outputs 3 and 4 when the device has them. The **mic** (`DjMicSection`) is the audio device's first input channel, handed to `DjEngine::process` by the booth's callback (`DjBooth::audioDeviceIOCallbackWithContext` in `SessionDj.cpp`): ±12 dB shelves and three modes, off, on, and talkover, which ducks the master by 18 dB while the mic is spoken into and lets it back up as the mic falls quiet. The engine's three-argument `process` passes no inputs, and the callback called that form until the wiki keeper's check of 2026-10-09 caught it: the engine's own test drove the overload and could not tell. `Session::djProcessOffline` now drives the booth through the very same callback, and `DjBooth.inc` feeds a tone through it and reads the mic's meter. The **send/return unit** (`DjSendFx`) takes the channels' sends: a short delay (5-120 ms), a long delay, a dub echo (whose repeats lose their bottom as well as their top) or a reverb, with size (feedback, or decay), time (a delay's time glides, a thousandth of the way each sample, so a knob turn bends rather than clicks; a test measuring the return runs about 100 blocks after setting the type, or the click comes back early), tone and the level the return comes back into the master at.

`DjBeatFx` is one unit on one channel or the master, timed in beats of the master tempo, or of the tapped tempo (`DjEngine::tapBpm`, 128 until the TAP key is pressed) when nothing plays, or by its own TIME knob with Auto off. Fourteen kinds: delay and echo (both ring out after they are switched off), ping-pong, spiral and helix (feedback delays whose repeats climb two and seven semitones through `DjPitchShifter`), reverb and shimmer, flanger, phaser, a swept filter, trans (a gate), roll (captures the beats after it is switched on and repeats them), pitch (−12 to +12 whole semitones over the depth knob) and vinyl brake (the sound winds down to nothing over the beat). The band keys (`bandLow`, `bandMid`, `bandHigh`, the DJM's FX FREQUENCY) keep the dry signal in the bands switched off: the effect's change is split by a three-band isolator and only the lit bands' share is added back, so a tone in a dark band passes untouched. The swept effects take their phase from the master's beat count, so they lock to the beat.

## What a deck plays

- **A file** is read whole and analysed on the booth's one worker thread, up to fifteen minutes, and `Session::djPoll` installs it. Any format JUCE's basic formats read.
- **A track or a group of the song** is bounced to audio on the same worker, against a copy of the document loaded from a snapshot of its state rather than the live edit; why, the ownership rules that keep the copy safe, the sixty-second budget and the test runners' inline render are in [A deck's bounce renders on a worker against a copy of the document](dj-bounce-on-a-copy.md). The render is a merge's ([Arrangement view](arrangement-view.md)): from the top of the song to the end of the last clip among the tracks, in whole bars, through the tracks' own devices and faders and not the main chain, solo elsewhere and slot clips kept out, the bus named with the members that feed it (a bus alone renders nothing). The bounce lands with a loop over its whole length, so a stem plays round. It knows its tempo exactly, so the grid is laid from the song's. A one-bar bounce took 0.6-1 s on 2026-10-09.
- **A bounced deck goes stale** on every document change (`markModified` calls `djDocumentChanged`; a change that names its track, as a knob move does, marks only the decks playing that track or a group it is in), and the poll bounces it again once the document has been quiet for half a second, one deck per poll, swapping the new audio in by beat. That is how the lower pane edits a deck while it plays: a deck's Edit key opens its track's clip, in the note editor for a MIDI track and the audio editor for an audio one (`Session::djDeckEditClip`: the cell the deck plays, else the first clip of the track's kind, earliest first; the control bar's Clip toggle opens the same clip, see [App shell and control bar](app-shell.md)). Reload bounces at once; a right-click on it switches the automatic re-bounce off for that deck.
- **Live** switches a track deck's monitoring On ([Track inputs and monitoring](inputs-and-monitoring.md)), so its instrument plays from the keys over the bounce; a Drum Rack on it plays its pads. The monitoring is put back when Live goes off. A group has no instrument and a file no track, so both refuse, as do an audio track and a MIDI track running no instrument. A refusal from arming (no audio device, an input the machine lacks) leaves the deck live all the same: the track took the setting and will be heard once there is an input.
- **Removing a deck closes the gap** (`removeDjDeck`): the decks above move down with their settings and channel strips, but material cannot move between engine decks without a copy, so each deck that moved is marked stale and the poll bounces or reads it again where it landed, with its cue, hot cues and loop as they stood.

## The analysis

`analyseDjTrack` makes the picture and the numbers from a float buffer. One pass fills a waveform column per 256 frames in three bands (lows under 180 Hz, highs over 2.5 kHz) and, per column, the energy of four bands. The tempo comes from an onset envelope (log-compressed rises of the band energies, local mean removed) by autocorrelation over 60-200 BPM, scored with its octaves and a mild preference around 128, then a comb search that settles the period to five thousandths of a BPM and finds the phase. The band energies for that envelope are averaged over 46 ms: a column is shorter than one cycle of a kick, so a column's own energy rose and fell with the sine's phase and the ripple correlated with itself every 0.4 beats and buried the beat (the analyser read 158.2 BPM for a synthesised 126.5 BPM beat; a Python replica of the arithmetic, printing the correlation curve, is what showed the ripple, see [Measure sound, don't read the DSP](measure-sound-dont-read-dsp.md)). The phase is then read from the unsmoothed flux, which spikes in the column each attack lands in. The downbeat is the beat of the four the low band hits hardest. Bars are measured as RMS from the first downbeat, and a drop is a bar that is loud, half again as loud as the four before it and plainly louder than the one before, eight bars apart at least. The key is a chroma profile (a 4096-point transform every 8192 frames, 55 Hz to 2.2 kHz, lower partials weighted) against the Krumhansl-Schmuckler profiles, named as a DJ reads it with its Camelot number.

## The view

`DjView` stands the decks either side of the mixer, odd to the left and even to the right, up to three a column. **It syncs the mixer before it lays the decks out**, because the layout reads the mixer's width from its strips: laid out first, a fourth deck's strip sat under the master section until the window was next resized (reported 2026-10-09; [A layout that reads a child's preferred size must sync the child first](sync-children-before-layout.md)).

`DjDeckPanel` is laid out as a CDJ-3000 is. The name bar chooses the source (the song's tracks and groups, a file chooser, the library's samples by pack, the machine's drives) or takes a dropped file, and on a deck playing a track of the song carries Live, Edit and Reload. Under it the screen, then the eight hot cues. The body is three columns. To the left: the loop keys (in, out, reloop/exit, the beat loop with its length on a right-click, halve and double), the beat jump with its size, cue search, Reverse and the deck's quantize key, with the big round Cue and Play beneath. In the middle the **jog wheel** (`DjJogWheel` in `DjControls.h`): a platter whose marker turns with the track at 33⅓ rpm, a ring of ticks, the track's name in the window, and a vinyl-mode key and the brake knob under it. In vinyl mode a hand on the platter scratches — the hand's angular speed, in turns of 1.8 s, becomes the deck's rate, and a hand that rests holds the record still, a 30 Hz timer noticing the rest — and a standing deck is scrubbed; the ring, or the platter in CDJ mode, nudges the tempo. To the right Sync, Master, the tempo range, the tempo fader (the CDJ's way, down is faster, with a centre detent and a scale) and Reset. The screen takes about a third of the console and no more than 220 px since 2026-10-10 (it took whatever the keys left, which on a tall window was most of the console); the body - the keys, the jog wheel and the clips grid - takes the rest. A short console squeezes its key rows before it touches the screen, and a short or narrow one drops the jog wheel first, giving it and its two controls empty bounds so the test that wants every key inside the console still holds. Every key with a second action on a right-click (a hot cue, Reload, Eject, Beat Loop) is a `DjPad`, never a stock button ([A juce::Button fires its click on a right-click too](juce-button-fires-on-right-click.md)). A press anywhere on the console focuses its deck: the panel is its own deep mouse listener and drops the second hearing of a direct press by event time ([A JUCE component listening to itself hears its own clicks twice](juce-self-listener-hears-clicks-twice.md)). The focused deck wears the selection strip and a lit number in its name bar; `DjView::focusDeck` marks it and tells the shell (`deckSelected` carries the deck's index), the shell's lower pane follows its track ([App shell and control bar](app-shell.md)), and `togglePlayFocused` is what Space does in this view, where the song's transport is dimmed.

`DjMixerPanel` keeps the mixer at one height (`preferredHeight`, 560 px, top-aligned in the middle of the view), as the hardware does; its width follows its strips, which narrow when six of them and the master section would not fit half the view. A strip is, top to bottom, what the V10's channel is: TRIM, COMP, HI, HI MID, LOW MID, LOW, FILTER, SEND, the CUE and FX keys, the meter beside the fader, and the A/THRU/B key. The master section is three columns: the master level and isolator with the stereo meters, the filters' resonance and the channel-fader curve; the headphones (mix, level, mono split) and the mic (level, two shelves, off/on/talkover, a meter); the send unit (type, size, time, tone, mix) and the beat effect (type, target, the three band keys, the beat between ◀ and ▶, AUTO and TAP, TIME and DEPTH, ON/OFF). The tempo readout at the top of the master column shows the master deck's BPM, or the tapped tempo when nothing plays, repainted only when its text changes. `DjDeckDisplay` scrolls the waveform under a fixed playhead from tiles painted once at the current zoom (the wheel zooms, 3-48 s across), draws the grid, the loop and the cue flags on top each frame, shows the whole track in a strip below with its drops marked and the played part dimmed (a click seeks), and a line of readouts repainted only when their text changes. Dragging the scrolling strip pushes the tempo while the button is down, as a hand on the platter does. The view's displays move on the display's refresh, its keys and meters at 30 Hz, and the shell's timer polls the loads. Hidden, the view marks itself stale as the other faces do ([What a change costs the interface](ui-cost-of-a-change.md)). Meaning colours are `palette::dj*` in `Theme.h`. A deck's remove key is answered through `MessageManager::callAsync` (`DjView::removeDeck`), because the request comes from a key on the panel the removal destroys.

## Live preview while a knob is held

A bounced deck is heard a bounce late: a knob on the track's device was heard only after half a second's quiet and a render, where the arrangement runs its devices live. So while a knob on a track's device is held (`Session::beginDeviceParameterGesture`), every deck playing that track - a MIDI track running an instrument, playing or waiting - is heard through the track itself (`djBeginLivePreview`): Live is switched on for it, as the Live key does, and the engine fades the deck's bounce out over five milliseconds (`DjEngine::previewGains`, a `DjSmoothedGain` per deck; a step to silence and back was a click at each end of every knob touch, reported 2026-10-10) and plays the bounce's notes into the same MIDI inputs the typing keyboard plays into (`DjEngine::sequence`, `DjLiveSink`, the booth's `noteOn`/`noteOff` into the inputs' keyboard state from the audio thread, whose lock is brief and whose queue is made for a driver thread). The notes are `DjTrack::midi`, gathered on the message thread when the bounce is made: a slot clip's own, or every MIDI clip of the track where it lies, in beats from the top, with the clip's content offset taken off; a group is not previewed. The sequencer runs once a block per deck: notes go out as the deck passes their beats, a loop plays them round (the loop's end is played out before the loop starts again), a jump or a stop lets every held note go and replays nothing from before the landing, and so does the preview ending. The deck keeps its own transport throughout, so its tempo, loops and cues still hold; what it loses for the moment is the mixer strip, since the live track plays to the song's output. When the knob is let go (`djEndLivePreview`, at the last gesture's end) the deck bounces again on the worker at once and keeps previewing until that bounce lands or fails (`finishDjLoad` ends the preview either way, before installing: it puts Live back as it was and panics the track's MIDI for anything the engine had not let go of), so the sound never drops. The poll never bounces a deck while it previews (`djPoll` skips it); a Reload or a cell change still does, and its landing ends the preview like any other. A parameter change names its track (`markModified(track)`, `djDocumentChanged(track)`), so only the decks playing that track, or a group it is in, go stale for it; other edits still mark every deck. One trap: `djBeginLivePreview` switches Live on through `setDjDeckLive` and then reads the deck's `live` flag, not the call's Result. The Result reports a refusal from arming (no audio device, an input the machine lacks) while the deck is live all the same, and the first version, which read the Result, never began the preview in the test's second document.

## The clips grid

Each console carries its track's clip slots as a column of cells (`DjClipGrid`, the clips the paused session view would show for that track), under a SONG cell that is the track's arrangement. The column stands beside the big Cue and Play keys when the console's left column is at least the key size plus 70 px wide, else above them, and takes empty bounds when not even one cell fits, for the test that reads every child ([Writing Rhino tests](writing-rhino-tests.md)). A deck plays one cell at a time: SONG bounces the arrangement as before, and a slot cell bounces that one clip through the track's chain (`Session::loadDjDeckSlot`, `DjDeckInfo::slot`, saved as the deck's `slot`): the bounce lays the slot's clip on the copy's timeline from the top in place of the arrangement's clips, drops its loop and renders the clip's own length, and a deck already playing that track keeps its beat across the swap. The lit cell is the one the deck plays. A press on a filled cell loads it; a double-click on an empty cell makes a one-bar MIDI clip there (`createSlotClip`, named for its cell; an audio track's cell refuses with a hint to drop a sample), loads it and opens it in the note editor, so the notes go in while the deck turns and the deck bounces again after each change; a double-click on a filled cell opens it; the row past the last scene makes a scene. A right-click deletes a clip, and a deck that played it goes back to SONG; the delete repairs the note editor's clip (`repairPatternClip`, as the timeline's delete does, and as a copy over a filled cell and a sample dropped on one now do), because a console opens a slot clip there and the editor painted a freed clip otherwise - the crash of 2026-10-10 ([Hold ids, not pointers](ids-not-pointers.md)). A copy into a cell takes a name no other clip on the track wears, slots and timeline alike (`uniqueClipNameOnTrack`: a copy of "Clip 1" beside it is "Clip 2", of "Break" is "Break 2"; `copyClipInto` sets the name again after the MIDI clone, which carries the source's name across), and a new cell's "Clip N" is made unique the same way. A MIDI track with no instrument cannot be rendered, SONG or cell alike - the engine's `RenderTask` fails at once with "Didn't find any audio to render" - so `bounceDjDeck` hands the deck silence of the span on the song's grid instead (checked after the no-clips refusal, so a track with nothing to play at all still refuses), and the instrument's arrival bounces it again, as any edit does; a cell made on a bare track used to fail to load and leave the deck's keys dark, the second bug of 2026-10-10.

Clips are separate from the arrangement's, as the session view's are, and every route across copies (`ClipDrag.h` carries the descriptions). A cell dragged leaves as `rhino-slot:<track>:<scene>` with a small tile for a drag image, and lands on another console's cell (`copyClipToTrackSlot`, held to the lane's kind) or, through the Arrange switch, on a lane of the timeline (`copySlotClipToArrangement` with a target lane). A timeline clip carried up out of the arrangement, past the band where the lanes scroll, leaves as `rhino-clip:<id>` - the arrangement cancels its own move first, so the clip stays put - and lands on a cell as a copy. The control bar's two view toggles are drop targets (`ViewToggle`) that show their view while a clip or a browser item hovers them, which is how a drag crosses. Library items land on a cell by the track's kind, as on a lane: a sound file, a library sample among them, makes an audio clip on an audio track and is refused on a MIDI track, except that a track running a Drum Rack takes it onto a pad (`rhino-browser:file:` only; a built-in `sample:` goes to `insertBuiltInSampleInSlot` and is refused on any MIDI track); a pattern makes a MIDI clip; and an instrument, effect, preset or kit joins the track's chain without making a clip. A drop on the console beside the grid goes into the first free cell and plays. A deck playing a file or a group has no track of the song: its grid is hidden and a dropped file is its material, as before.

`Session::findClip` finds a clip in a slot as well as on the timeline since 2026-10-10, which is what lets the note editor and the audio editor open a slot clip, and `trackHoldingClip` (`SessionInternal.h`) answers for the track of a clip whose own lookup knows only the timeline. [Session view (paused)](session-view.md) records the consequence.

## What the document saves

`rhinoDj` on the edit's state, written when a snapshot is taken (`projectSnapshot`) and read back on restore: each deck's source (a file by stored path, a track by its `EditItemID` with the `slot` it plays, −1 for the arrangement, a group by id), tempo settings, brake and quantize, cue, hot cues and loop in seconds, Live and the automatic re-bounce; every channel strip, compressor, four bands, send and fader curve included; the crossfader and its curve; the master, the headphones' level and mono split; the mic; the send unit; the effect with its bands, time mode and manual time; the tapped tempo; quantise and phase lock. Positions are not saved. A reopened deck is empty and stale and the poll loads it again; its cues are applied once the material is there (`applyDjDeckSettings`). Adding, removing or ejecting a deck, loading a file, a track or a group (the track and group loads only since the second pass of 2026-10-09; `363fd05` missed them), setting or clearing a hot cue and changing quantise mark the document modified; knob and fader moves do not, and nothing here is an undo step. Those marks go through `markDjModified`, which bumps the revision and the edit's changed flag without `markModified`: `markModified` calls `djDocumentChanged`, which marks every bounced deck stale, and a hot cue set must not re-bounce the deck it was set on.

## Decisions

- **Decks play audio, not the edit.** A deck with a transport of its own cannot be a Tracktion track driven by the song's one transport, so the song's material reaches a deck by being bounced. The price is the re-bounce after an edit, which the half-second quiet period and the swap by beat make tolerable.
- **One engine, one callback,** rather than a Tracktion edit per deck: a second edit per deck would have cost a graph each and still needed the mixer written.
- **Vinyl-style tempo** (pitch moves with speed) for now; a pitch-held mode needs the engine's stretcher on the booth's thread and is noted as not built.
- **Rhino's own mixer DSP** rather than the edit's plugins, so the mixer is measured offline and never rebuilds a playback graph.
- **A bare MIDI track bounces as silence, not a refusal** (2026-10-10). The console's flow is make a cell, then add the instrument, then draw; a refusal left the deck dark with no way on but Reload, while silence loads at once and the instrument's arrival re-bounces it like any other edit.
- **A previewed deck plays to the song's output, not through its channel strip** (2026-10-10). The user's rule was to favour the sound: a held knob must be heard in real time and the re-render belongs on a parallel process once it is let go. The live sound is the track's own monitoring, so while the knob is held that deck's strip, cue, meters and effects are silent; accepted as a simulation until the knob is dropped, with a tap of the track's live output into the strip as the next step.

## Not built

Key lock; slip mode; a sampler; recording the master; MIDI control of the booth; a chooser for the mic's input (the booth takes the device's first input channel); the booth's own output routing (booth out, the V10's second output); a bounce that costs the message thread nothing (the copy it starts from is still made there, a fraction of a second); a tap of a previewed track's live output into its deck's channel strip, so a held knob is heard through the mixer rather than beside it.
