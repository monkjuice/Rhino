# Pointer and selection rules

The arrangement and the note editor answer the pointer the same way, because a clip and a note are the same kind of thing to select. `native/src/ui/shared/SelectionInput.h` writes the rule down as shared predicates rather than a shared gesture; each panel still decides what its marquee catches.

## The rules

- **A plain press on an item** takes it and drops everything else. In the arrangement, since 2026-10-23, that is the clip's own span: a press on a header the selection does not touch, or on either trim handle, selects exactly that clip, because a trim edits one clip, never a selection ([Region editing](region-editing.md#one-selection)).
- **A plain press on empty space** sweeps out a region or a marquee, and clears the old selection if it never travels. In the arrangement a clip's lower row is empty space too; a region swept across part of a clip selects that part (the clip counts as selected) and, since 2026-10-24, makes the clip the press landed in, or the nearest, the primary one on release, so its editor shows it ([Region editing](region-editing.md#one-selection)); dragging any header inside the region carries all of it ([Region editing](region-editing.md#moving-a-selection)). The region's edges and the insert point snap to the grid and, since commit `c1e61d1` (2026-10-26), to the starts and ends of clips on the rows the region spans: a press within 14 px short of an off-grid clip start, or past its trim handle in the same cell, lands on the start, and a sweep overshooting a clip's end snaps back to it ([Arrangement view](arrangement-view.md#rules-worth-knowing)).
- **Ctrl** (`isToggleSelectionModifier`, Ctrl or Command) adds one item or takes it back out. **Shift** extends, which for a note grid degrades to adding, and never removes. On a clip header the two mean the same thing since 2026-10-23: both widen the rectangle to take the clip in (`extendSelectionToClip`) and make it the primary clip, because a rectangle has nothing to toggle out and Live's arrangement has only Shift-extend for clips; a clip already inside stays inside. The exception is Shift on a warped audio clip's trim handle, which stretches the clip (commit `4193cdf`, [Time warp](time-warp.md#stretching-a-clip-by-its-edge)); on an unwarped clip's handle Shift still widens. A Shift press on a lane widens from the corner of the rectangle farthest from the pointer, so extending works after a clip click too, and `regionExtending` keeps it a span before it travels: it used to widen on the press and collapse back to a line on release, because the release path treated an untravelled press as a line, found while writing the Shift-click check in `RegionClipboard.inc`. Track cards are the exception: they are a list, so Shift selects the range between two cards. A click on a note editor key selects every note of its pitch in the clip, with Shift adding them and Ctrl taking them out when all are already selected, adding them otherwise (commit `f2ae477`; Live toggles with Shift there, but Ctrl is the grid's toggle).
- **A modifier press is about the selection only** (`isMultiSelectModifier`). It must never also start carrying, or adding one clip to a group would nudge the whole group.
- **A press inside a multi-selection keeps it** so the drag can carry all of it, and collapses onto the item under the pointer on release if no drag happened (`collapseSelectionOnRelease`). That cannot be decided on the press. In the arrangement overlap decides, not the press point: a press on the header of a clip the selection touches, even outside the covered part of a partially covered clip, keeps the selection and carries it, and collapses onto the clip on an untravelled release only when the selection was wider than the clip (`TimeSelection::isExactly`). A move drag is clamped so the rectangle's first row stays at or below track 0 and its start at or after the song's start; the carried rows show Live's circle-with-slash where their material could not land; on release one `Session::moveClipRegion` moves everything, the selection follows it (`TimeSelection::shifted`) and the primary clip is kept ([Arrangement view](arrangement-view.md#rules-worth-knowing)). After any change the selection follows the clip it is exactly - a nudged, trimmed or undone clip takes its own-span selection with it, and a deleted one clears it - while a wider rectangle stays where it was put ([Region editing](region-editing.md#one-selection)).
- **Left and Right nudge the selection's material** by a grid step, Shift by a bar, whenever the selection is a range ([Region editing](region-editing.md#the-operations)). In the note editor they move the selected notes a step, Alt a sixteenth of one, and Shift+Left/Right resizes them (since commit `4783232`, 2026-10-25).
- **Creating is a double-click** in both editors: an empty lane makes a clip filling the grid cell the pointer is inside (`snappedDown`, as any pointer position takes), never shorter than a bar ([Arrangement view](arrangement-view.md); always one bar until commit `3a0933c`), an empty cell a note, which Alt puts where the pointer is rather than on its step (commit `f2ae477`). The single click is then free to mean "select" everywhere.
- **Drawing is a mode, not a modifier**: B (or the footer button) toggles `StepGrid::setDrawMode`, and only draw mode erases with the pointer.
- **The right button asks rather than edits.** It opens a menu of what can be done to what it landed on (a clip, a card, a note or its velocity marker, the empty grid), and a right-click inside a selection keeps it, so the menu acts on all of it ([Region editing](region-editing.md#one-selection)). In the note editor an unselected note becomes the selection first, and empty space keeps the selection and moves the insert line to the click. The one exception is the arrangement's ruler, where it still clears the loop; since commit `f2ae477` the note editor's ruler opens a LOOP menu instead (Loop Clip, Double Loop Length, Clear Loop), and whether the arrangement's should follow was put to the user. The note editor's right button erased, in either mode, until commit `4783232` (2026-10-25), so every right-click meant as a question cost a note ([Note editor](note-editor.md#gestures)).
- **The wheel.** In the note editor Ctrl and the wheel always zoom, as Shift and the wheel do, and any wheel turn ends a Ctrl+E chop (commit `f2ae477`); the chop's parts are set by dragging with Ctrl held (commit `c6da530`). Until commit `4783232` they opened a divide, which chopped the selection for anyone reaching for the zoom, and until `f2ae477` they adjusted one already open. A sideways wheel or swipe scrolls along the clip, as the arrangement's does. Alt and the wheel, anywhere over the note editor, make its rows a pixel taller or shorter a notch, within 5 px of their own 13 (commit `3418c1b`); they used to scroll like the plain wheel.
- **Alt** during a drag bypasses snapping, in the arrangement the snap to clip edges included. In the note editor snapping became the default in commit `f2ae477`: a move lands the grabbed note on a step line once the pointer has gone half a step, and a resized edge lands on a line; Alt moves in sixteenths of a step, which a move did without it before, resizes continuously and is reapplied when pressed or let go with the pointer still.
- **Escape** cancels a drag and clears the time selection, and the **middle button** pans the arrangement from anywhere, headers included, and since commit `0725daa` the note editor from anywhere, keys and notes included: steps by the pixel, rows by whole rows, drum rows included since they scroll (commit `d872f0b`, 2026-10-25; before, they panned only sideways) ([Note editor](note-editor.md#gestures)).

The note editor's [velocity lane](note-velocity-lane.md) (2026-10-16) applies the same rules to its markers. A press takes a marker's note, Ctrl or Shift adds to the selection, a click inside a group collapses on release, and a press on empty lane starts a marquee that catches markers by their dot. Three things differ there. A drag arms only after 3 px, and arming moves nothing. A click is a press that never armed, not one that changed nothing: a group dragged against a rail moves no value but keeps its selection on release. B paints values rather than notes. Double-click does nothing, because a marker can stand for a note scrolled out of sight; the right button opens the marker's note's menu and erases and drags nothing. A second press while a lane gesture is held (a second finger, or a pen beside the mouse) ends that gesture before it starts another.

## Autoscroll

A drag reaching a panel's edge pulls the view after it, so a selection can sweep past the screen. `autoScrollPush` is the shared ramp: it starts 30 px inside the edge and grows to a cap of three scroll steps. It is driven by each panel's frame clock (the arrangement's `VBlankAttachment`, the note editor's gesture timer), never by mouse moves, because a pointer held still outside the panel sends no more drag events. Each panel scrolls in exactly one place: scrolling in the drag handler as well made a moving pointer travel at double speed. For the same reason the note editor's marquee anchors in steps and lines (the pitch on a piano clip, a row on a drum clip), not pixels, and asks the clip rather than the visible rows what it caught. Gestures in the velocity lane autoscroll in time only, never in pitch. A lane stroke is held to the visible steps, so a pointer parked past the edge paints the columns autoscroll brings in.

## History

These rules retired the held-key marquee, **S**, in both panels.
