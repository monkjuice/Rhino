# Region editing

In the arrangement's lanes every command acts on a **region**: a span of time across a run of tracks, not a set of clips, as in Live. The UI half is `native/src/ui/arrangement/ArrangementSelection.cpp`; the edit half is `Session::copyClipRegion`, `clearClipRegion` and `pasteClipRegion` in `native/src/model/SessionRegion.cpp` and `moveClipRegion` in `SessionRegionMove.cpp`. With a card focused, or from the card menu, the same keys copy, paste and delete whole tracks instead ([Track clipboard](track-clipboard.md)). The arrangement holds one clipboard of each kind and copying into either empties the other, so Ctrl+V always puts down what was copied last.

## One selection

Since commit `cff657d` (2026-10-23) the lanes have exactly one selection, `Arrangement::timeSelection`, a rectangle, as Live's arrangement has (Live 12 manual, section 6.9, "Selecting Clips and Time"). Clicking a clip's header selects the clip's own span on its track, exactly what a marquee swept over the clip would select (`TimeSelection::isExactly`); dragging over the lanes sweeps a span; Shift or Ctrl on a header widens the rectangle to the one holding both (`extendSelectionToClip`), and a Shift press on a lane extends from the corner farthest from the pointer. A clip the rectangle runs across, in part or whole, is selected (`TimeSelection::touches`, what `isSelected` answers); its outline lights only when the whole clip is held (`holdsWhole`). `selected` survives as the *primary* clip, the one the last header press took or the last finished sweep named (below): the lower pane shows it, a trim edits it, and the clip commands fall back to it when there is no range (`clipsToActOn`: R, mute, merge, bounce as a clip, split), but copy, cut, duplicate and delete never read it; `setSelection(ids, primary)` selects the one rectangle bounding those clips, read off the timeline's own list, so an id not on it selects nothing and an empty list clears the selection, insert point included. A click that never drags leaves a zero-width region, the *insert point*, where a paste lands and playback starts. Since commit `c1e61d1` (2026-10-26) a sweep's edges and the insert point snap to the starts and ends of clips on the rows the region spans as well as to the grid, so a press just short of a clip lands on its edge ([Arrangement view](arrangement-view.md#rules-worth-knowing)).

**A finished sweep names the primary** (2026-10-24, commit `4bd0f45`; under `cff657d` a sweep named none). The user asked that selecting part of a MIDI clip show that part in the note editor, as Live's Clip View does. `beginRegionGesture` leaves the primary alone at the press; on release `endRegionGesture` calls `nameSweptClip`, which picks, among the clips the span touches, the one the press landed in, or else the nearest to the press: the press's row first, then the time gap from `regionAnchorTime`. A Shift sweep that widened a selection keeps the primary it had while the span still runs across it; an insert point, or a span with no clip in it, names none. A MIDI primary becomes the note editor's clip through `Session::selectPatternClip` (skipped when it already is, to spare a synchronous broadcast), `clipSelected` fires if the primary changed, and `clipSpanSwept(primary, start, end)` reports the span, which the shell hands to the note editor ([Note editor](note-editor.md#showing-a-swept-span)). Audio clips follow the same rule, so an open audio clip editor follows the sweep rather than closing. A sweep cut short (`cancelDrag`: Escape, a key command, a document change mid-drag) names nothing, and clears the primary it kept, announcing `clipSelected({})`, if the span no longer touches it. The primary is decided on release, not cleared at the press, because the shell polls it on every change ([App shell](app-shell.md#the-lower-pane)): cleared at the press, it closed the editor at the first broadcast of the sweep.

**The selection follows the clip it is exactly.** Before `Arrangement::sync()` rebuilds the clip list it asks whether the rectangle is exactly the primary clip's own span (`isExactly` on the old list, the only place the clip's old span still is); if it is, after the rebuild the rectangle becomes that clip's new span, and it is cleared when the clip is gone, deleted or taken back by an undo. Any other selection - a swept span, a widened one, a pasted rectangle, one over several clips - only has its rows clamped and stays where it was put; the gestures that move its material move it themselves (a move sets the shifted rectangle, a trim re-selects the clip), which for a whole clip is the span the rule lands on anyway. It is a computed rule, not a stored mode: a swept span that happens to equal a clip exactly follows it too, indistinguishable from a click. The old clip-owned region did this through `regionFollowsClips`, and without it the audio clip editing scenario failed ("Duplicate places audio clips exactly after the source clip when space is available"): Right nudged the clip, undo put it back at 0 s, the rectangle stayed at 0.125 s, and Ctrl+D duplicated the stale span.

Until then `effectiveRegion` resolved a dragged span or else the bounding rectangle of a set of selected clips, Ctrl toggled a clip in and out of that set, and a dragged span selected no clip; `selectedClips`, `regionFollowsClips`, `setRegionFromSelectedClips` and `Focus::clip` are gone. One rectangle was chosen over a set of clips or a set of rectangles because it is Live's model: non-contiguous Ctrl gathering was dropped, and Ctrl means Shift on a clip header ([Pointer and selection rules](pointer-and-selection.md)).

The commands about whole clips - reverse (R), mute, merge (Ctrl+J) - read `Arrangement::clipsToActOn`: every clip the selection touches, falling back to the primary clip when there is no range around it (a ruler click leaves a point selection but keeps the primary). Bounce (Ctrl+B) bounces any range selection as a span, cut at its edges, which for a clicked clip is the clip whole ([Bounce to New Track](bounce-to-new-track.md#a-time-selection)). A right-click on a clip inside the region keeps the region. The colour key (C) acts on the primary clip alone, the one the last header press took: `Arrangement::keyPressed` calls `session.cycleClipColour(selected)` ([Track and clip colours](track-and-clip-colours.md)).

## The operations

- **Copy (Ctrl+C)** reads the selection rectangle and nothing else, as cut, duplicate and delete do. A point selection copies nothing ("Select clips or drag a span of the timeline to copy"), even while a primary clip lingers, as after a ruler click; the old `effectiveRegion()` fell back to the selected clips' span, so selecting a clip, putting the insert point elsewhere and pressing Ctrl+C still copied the clip. Nothing is lost in the app, because a lane click that puts the insert point down also clears the selection, so the copy always had to come first. It takes a `ClipRegion`: the rectangle's own width and track count, plus a `ClipSnapshot` per clip inside, cut at the edges. The size is carried rather than measured off the clips, because an empty lane or a trailing rest is part of what was copied. A clip that only grazes the region is not copied: one inside it by less than `minimumRegionPieceSeconds(span)` (`model/ClipGeometry.h`), which is 10 ms (`minimumSplitSeconds`) or a tenth of the region if that is shorter. A drum sample one sample longer than a beat reaches one sample across the next grid line, and copying the region after it used to paste that sample as a sliver that played nothing (commit `972cc0e`). The floor shrinks with the region because one grid cell at the deepest zoom is about 0.12 ms (1/16384 at 120 BPM, a few samples): at a fixed 10 ms, copying it copied nothing, so cutting it did nothing (commit `cf4e959`). From 0.1 s up it is the old 10 ms. A clip wholly inside the region is copied however short it is.
- **Delete** empties the region (`clearClipRegion`) whatever made it, so a clicked clip's own span deletes the clip and a swept span cuts a hole: split at both edges, then remove what lies wholly inside, so trimming, cutting a hole and deleting whole clips are one case. It leaves the insert point at the region's start (since 2026-10-23; before, deleting a clicked clip left no selection). A clip reaching past an edge by less than that same floor is not split but deleted whole, its tail outside included; paste's clear does the same. The cuts go through `splitClipAt` (declared in `SessionInternal.h`, shared with `SessionRegionMove.cpp`), because Tracktion refuses one within 1 ms of a clip's end; until `cf4e959` that refusal made Delete on a deepest-zoom cell inside a clip take everything from the cell to the clip's end ([Clips never overlap](clip-placement.md#one-place)). **Cut (Ctrl+X)** is copy, then delete.
- **Paste (Ctrl+V)** clears the whole destination rectangle, then inserts, so it replaces what it lands on. A row whose kind does not match its lane, or that lands on a group bus, refuses the whole paste before anything is cleared. A failure after that point (a source file gone, an instrument that will not load) takes back the whole transaction with `undoCurrentTransactionOnly`, cleared ground and added lanes included (commit `b5d17e0`). Lanes a paste adds below the last one get their fader, slots and routing at once. The pasted rectangle becomes the selection, so a second paste replaces the first.
- **Duplicate (Ctrl+D)** pastes at the region's own end and moves the region onto the copy, so holding Ctrl+D keeps extending.
- **Split (Ctrl+E)** cuts at the insert line: the selected clips it crosses if any, otherwise every clip it crosses on the region's tracks, so nothing needs selecting first.
- **Nudge (Left and Right, Shift for a bar)** moves the region's material by one grid step and the region with it (`Arrangement::nudgeSelected`, `Session::moveClipRegion`), Live's "nudge a selection of material": a swept span is cut at its edges and the inside moves, replacing what it covers. The arrows are taken whenever the selection is a range (until 2026-10-23 only with a clip selected, moving whole clips through `moveClips`).

## Moving a selection

Dragging any clip header the region runs across carries the whole region: the clips inside it whole, and of each clip crossing an edge the part inside, cut there. The user asked for it with two Live screenshots: a span across part of one MIDI clip plus two whole clips on the track above, dragged one row down by a header, with a forbidden mark on the audio row that could not take MIDI. The gesture is in `ArrangementGestures.cpp` ([Pointer and selection rules](pointer-and-selection.md)), the preview in pieces and the refusal mark are in the [Arrangement view](arrangement-view.md#rules-worth-knowing), and the edit is `Session::moveClipRegion(start, end, firstTrack, lastTrack, destinationStart, destinationTrack, moved*)` in `native/src/model/SessionRegionMove.cpp`, declared beside `pasteClipRegion`. It is cut and paste with the identities kept, chosen for consistency with `pasteClipRegion`:

- **One cut function.** `cutClipByRegion(clip, start, end)` in `model/ClipGeometry.h` returns a `RegionCut`: whether the clip is `inside`, the piece `taken` (its offset advanced by how far into the clip the piece starts), and `keepsLeft`/`keepsRight` with the `left`/`right` remainders. A clip not overlapping the region is not inside; a cropped clip whose part inside is shorter than `minimumRegionPieceSeconds` is left out, as a copy leaves it out; one crossing an edge by at least the floor is cut there, and one crossing by less goes whole, as a clear takes it whole. The arrangement's drag preview and the model cut by this one function, which is what makes the preview and the drop agree about every edge. Unit-tested in `tests/Arrangement/ClipGeometryTest.cpp` (both edges with offsets, a graze, a sub-floor crossing, a 0.5 ms deep-zoom cell, NaN and zero-width regions).
- **Refusals first.** Every row is checked before anything is cut, with paste's messages: MIDI onto an audio lane, audio onto a MIDI lane, anything onto a group bus. A row past the last track refuses nothing, because a lane will be made for it. Since `b7bd73c` a return track's row counts as past the last track, and the move, like a paste, makes its lanes above the returns ([Return tracks and sends](return-tracks.md)). An empty region, or a move to the same place, succeeds and does nothing.
- **One transaction, "Move selection".** Lanes are appended for rows past the last track, but only through the last row that carries material (a paste makes the whole rectangle), each of the kind its row wants and an empty row between them as audio. The clips crossing the start and end edges are split through `splitClipAt`, and each piece's position is read back from the engine rather than computed, so a reversed clip's window is whatever the split left. Then the whole destination rectangle is cleared with `clearClipRegionInEdit`, sparing the travelling pieces: an empty lane inside the selection empties the lane it lands on, as a paste does, and a selection can move by less than its own width. The pieces land through `moveClipsInEdit`, the loop `moveClips` also runs (`SessionClips.cpp`, `ClipMoveOutcome`), so a MIDI piece brings its track's instrument like any moved clip, and a refusal there rolls the whole transaction back with `undoCurrentTransactionOnly`. The source rows, the destination rows and the rows that gained devices are marked, and playback resumes as after `moveClips`.
- **Identities.** A clip carried whole keeps its id; a piece cut from a clip is a new clip. Tracktion's split returns the right-hand clip and the original keeps the left, so after a cut at the region's *start* the travelling piece is the new id and the original stays behind; the scenario asserts positions rather than ids for cut pieces.

`tests/Arrangement/scenarios/RegionMove.inc` ("one selection, carried whole", right after the region clipboard scenario in `WorkflowTest.cpp`) drags a span across part of a MIDI clip and a whole audio clip two rows down and a beat right on the starter stack, checks the three-piece preview and the drop (the whole clip keeps its id and stays primary, the lane gained an instrument, one undo restores everything), the refusal of a one-row move with its mark on both rows and nothing cut, the arrow nudge of a swept span, lanes made past the end and undone, a move by less than the selection's width, an empty row that empties the lane it lands on, and an empty span that moves nothing. Since 2026-10-24 its sweep must also name the MIDI clip primary and pattern clip and report its span to a `StepGrid` ([Note editor](note-editor.md#showing-a-swept-span)), and `RegionClipboard.inc` asserts that the swept clip becomes the primary one, where it asserted none.

## Why snapshots, not ids

The clipboard holds values (`Session::ClipSnapshot`): position, offset and `rhinoClipColour`, plus, for a MIDI clip, the whole note list and its track's instrument as a catalog id. For an audio clip it holds the source file and speed, and since commit `b5d17e0` also the clip's gain, pan, pitch, fades, mute, reverse and warp (mode, clip tempo in beats, markers). Before that a copy played the raw file at speed one instead of what the original played. The paste writes reverse only for a clip that was reversed, and before anything else: writing it at all mirrors the new clip's window into its file, which made trimmed copies quieter until commit `50d87d5` ([Writing a clip's reverse switch moves its window](writing-reverse-moves-a-clips-window.md)). A cut deletes its sources, and an undo between copy and paste can too, so a clipboard of ids would paste nothing ([Hold ids, not pointers](ids-not-pointers.md)).

The clipboard also survives opening another document. Its files are full paths, so audio still resolves, but its clips are seconds from the region's corner, so pasted at another tempo they leave their beats. Open as of `c790287`, which fixed the same problem for the [Track clipboard](track-clipboard.md#into-another-document).

## In the note editor

`StepGridEditing.cpp` applies the same rule in steps: the marquee's span, the selected notes rounded out to whole steps, or the whole clip after Ctrl+A. Two differences. A note paste clears only the pitches and steps it writes, not the whole rectangle. And since commit `4783232` (2026-10-25) it lands on the editor's insert line, which a copy moves to the end of the copied region, so a copy goes down beside its source and pastes in a row lie end to end. In the lanes a paste lands at the selection's start, which after a copy is the source itself, and a second paste replaces the first. In the note editor that rule wrote a copy of selected notes over its source and removed the source, so nothing showed and dragging the "copy" away left a hole ([Note editor](note-editor.md#the-step-region-and-the-insert-line)). The arrangement was not changed. Ctrl+D leaves the clipboard alone in both.
